P: Most dental and health practices carry a quiet backlog: patients who are overdue for a check-up, a hygiene visit or a follow-up and simply have not booked. Nothing is wrong; life got busy. But regular visits matter for patients' health and are the backbone of a practice's schedule, so a systematic, friendly approach to recall is worth the effort. The difficulty is that recall is unglamorous work. It needs a list, a message, a way to book and follow-up, and front desks are already busy answering the phone. This guide sets out a practical approach: how to identify who is due, how to write respectful messages that do not pressure or make claims about health, how to make rebooking a one-step action, what questions patients ask when they respond, how a website chat assistant helps, and the privacy rules that apply to health information. Crescelo does not send recall campaigns itself; it answers questions and books appointments once the patient responds. That division is explained clearly below.
H2[step-1-know-who-is-due]: Step 1: Know who is due
TABLE:
H: Group | How to find them | Priority
R: Overdue for a routine check-up or hygiene visit | Your practice management system's recall list | High
R: Patients with an unfinished treatment plan | Treatment records | High, handled by clinical staff
R: Patients who missed and did not rebook | Appointment history | Medium
R: Long-lapsed patients | No visit in a year or two | Medium, with a gentler message
R: New patients who had one visit only | Appointment history | Medium
P: Your existing practice software usually produces these lists. Start with a manageable batch, such as the fifty most overdue, and work through it steadily instead of sending a flood.
H2[step-2-write-messages-patients-respond-to]: Step 2: Write messages patients respond to
UL:
- Use their name and the practice's name.
- State the reason simply: 'It has been a while since your last visit and you are due for a check-up.'
- Keep any health statements general and accurate; do not frighten or make promises about outcomes.
- Include one clear action: a booking link or a number.
- Offer flexibility: evening or early appointments, if you have them.
- Make it easy to say no: 'If you have moved or no longer wish to hear from us, reply to let us know.'
TABLE:
H: Message | Example
R: First reminder | Hi Sam, it is Riverside Dental. It has been a while since your last check-up, and you are due a visit. You can pick a time here: [link]. If you would rather call, we are on 0123 456 789.
R: Second reminder, two weeks later | Hi Sam, a quick reminder that you are due a check-up at Riverside Dental. We have some appointments free next week: [link].
R: Final, gentle | Hi Sam, we have not heard from you, so we will pause our reminders. Whenever you are ready, you can book here: [link].
P: Three messages spread over a month are enough. More becomes nagging.
H2[step-3-make-rebooking-one-step]: Step 3: Make rebooking one step
OL:
- Include a direct booking link or invite them to open the website chat.
- Show real available times, not a form that waits for a call back.
- Confirm immediately by email with the date, time, location and a private link to change or cancel.
- Remind them before the appointment; Crescelo sends email reminders for bookings made through its booking flow.
- Offer to book the next recall at the end of each visit, which prevents the backlog forming again.
P: The easiest recall is the one booked before the patient leaves the chair.
H2[step-4-prepare-for-the-questions-that-follow]: Step 4: Prepare for the questions that follow
P: When patients respond, they often ask practical questions. Have the answers in your knowledge base so the assistant can respond at any hour:
TABLE:
H: Question | What to have ready
R: How long will the visit take? | Typical duration of a check-up and hygiene visit
R: How much will it cost? | Prices or ranges, and what insurance or plans you accept; see [preparing your service menu and price list](/blog/prepare-service-menu-pricing-docs-rag)
R: Do you have evening or Saturday times? | Opening hours by day
R: I am nervous. Can you help? | How you support anxious patients, in warm, plain language
R: Do I need to bring anything? | Forms, previous records, insurance details
R: Where do I park? | Address, parking and transport details
P: Ensure the assistant does not give clinical advice or diagnose. It should explain that clinical questions are for the dentist and offer a booking or a call back.
H2[step-5-use-the-website-assistant-sensibly]: Step 5: Use the website assistant sensibly
UL:
- It answers routine questions about hours, prices, location and what to expect.
- It books an appointment when the patient is ready.
- It captures a name and contact details for a call back when a person is needed.
- It hands over sensitive or urgent situations, such as pain or swelling, with a clear message directing them to call the practice or emergency services as appropriate.
P: Write the escalation wording with your clinical team, and test it. See [when to hand over to a human](/blog/live-human-handover-flow-design).
H2[privacy-and-consent]: Privacy and consent
UL:
- Health-related information is sensitive personal data in many places, with extra obligations.
- Do not put clinical details in messages or in a chat that the assistant might store.
- Keep messages to what is necessary: an appointment is due.
- Follow your local rules on marketing consent and patient communications; recall reminders about care are often treated differently from promotions, but rules vary.
- Give patients a simple way to opt out, and honour it.
- Read the [GDPR checklist for website chatbots](/blog/website-chatbot-gdpr-checklist) and check the rules that apply to your profession and country.
P: This is general information, not legal advice.

P: Review monthly and adjust wording, timing and batch size.
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Sending a large batch all at once.
- Using clinical or alarming language.
- Not offering a quick way to book.
- Forgetting to book the next recall at each visit.
- Ignoring opt-outs.
- Expecting the chat assistant to send the campaign itself.
H2[a-four-week-recall-plan]: A four-week recall plan
TABLE:
H: Week | Action
R: 1 | Export the overdue list, remove anyone who has opted out or moved, and pick the first batch
R: 2 | Send the first message; have the front desk ready for replies and bookings
R: 3 | Send the second reminder to those who have not responded; review the questions patients asked and update the knowledge base
R: 4 | Send the final gentle message, count results and choose the next batch
P: Working in weekly batches keeps the schedule manageable, so new bookings do not arrive all at once, and it lets you improve the wording as you learn what patients respond to.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: How do I reduce a recall backlog?
A: Identify overdue patients from your records, send a short sequence of friendly messages with a one-step booking link and follow up in manageable batches.
Q: Can Crescelo send recall messages?
A: No. It does not send campaigns. You send them from your practice software or email tool, and Crescelo answers patient questions and books appointments.
Q: How many reminders should I send?
A: Two or three over about a month, then pause.
Q: What should the assistant not do?
A: Give clinical advice or diagnoses. It should offer a booking or direct the patient to a clinician.
Q: Is patient data safe in chat?
A: Keep chat to non-clinical information, follow local privacy law and use a provider with a clear data processing agreement.
Q: How do I prevent future backlogs?
A: Book the next recall appointment before the patient leaves.
