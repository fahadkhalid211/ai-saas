CLUSTER: Professional Services

P: Financial advice is a relationship business built on trust and heavily shaped by regulation. Prospects want to know whether an adviser can help them, how the adviser is paid and what a first meeting involves, and they want answers quickly, often outside office hours. A website chat can provide those answers and book an introductory meeting. But the same regulation that makes trust important makes automated communication risky: anything that looks like personalised advice or a recommendation can breach the rules, and communications may need to be approved, recorded and kept. This guide explains how an advisory firm can use chat carefully: what to allow and forbid, how to handle disclosure, what to collect, how to book consultations and how to work with compliance so the assistant is an asset rather than a liability.
NOTE: Financial services regulation varies by country and by the type of firm. This is general information, not legal or compliance advice. Involve your compliance officer from the start, and check your regulator's rules on advertising, electronic communications and record keeping.
H2[what-prospects-ask]: What prospects ask
TABLE:
H: Question | What the chat can do
R: What do you do and who do you help? | Describe services and client types in approved language
R: How are you paid? | State your fee model as approved: fees, commissions or both
R: What is a first meeting like? | Explain length, cost and what to prepare
R: Should I invest in X? | Not answer; explain that advice is given by qualified advisers
R: What return can I expect? | Not answer; explain that you do not predict or promise returns
R: Can you help with retirement, mortgages, insurance? | Say what you offer, and route to the right adviser
R: Are you regulated? | State your regulatory status and how to verify it
H2[the-line-between-information-and-advice]: The line between information and advice
TABLE:
H: Allowed | Not allowed
R: Describing services and the process | Recommending an investment, product or strategy
R: Explaining general concepts in neutral terms | Suggesting what a person should do with their money
R: Stating fees and status as approved | Quoting performance or predicting returns
R: Booking an introductory meeting | Commenting on an individual's circumstances
R: Directing to your disclosures | Guaranteeing results
P: Write these boundaries into the assistant's instructions, and test them with tempting questions such as 'Should I put my savings in property or stocks?'
H2[disclosure-and-required-statements]: Disclosure and required statements
UL:
- Say that it is an AI assistant and cannot give advice.
- State that advice is given by authorised advisers at a consultation.
- Include statements your regulator requires on risk, status and how you are paid, as your compliance officer decides.
- Link to your privacy policy and your regulatory disclosures.
- Make clear that using the chat does not create an advisory relationship.
P: Have compliance approve the greeting, the disclosure lines and any standard statements the assistant may use. See [AI transparency and disclosure](/blog/ai-transparency-disclosure-building-trust).
H2[what-to-collect]: What to collect
TABLE:
H: Field | Purpose
R: Name and contact method | Reply
R: Area of interest: retirement, investments, protection, business, other | Route to the right adviser
R: Timeframe | Priority
R: Preferred meeting time | Booking

P: Do not collect account numbers, national identifiers, detailed financial positions or documents in an open chat. Tell prospects the adviser will explain what is needed during an appropriate process. See the [GDPR checklist for website chatbots](/blog/website-chatbot-gdpr-checklist).
H2[book-the-introductory-meeting]: Book the introductory meeting
OL:
- Explain the meeting: length, format, cost or no cost, and what the adviser will cover.
- Say what to bring or think about, without asking for figures.
- Offer real times, including evenings if you have them.
- Confirm by email with the adviser's name, their credentials and how to change the time.
- Send a reminder.

H2[record-keeping-and-approval]: Record keeping and approval
UL:
- Ask compliance whether chat transcripts count as records you must keep, and for how long.
- Store transcripts securely with limited access, and delete them at the end of the retention period.
- Keep versions of the assistant's instructions and documents, with dates, so you can show what it was allowed to say.
- Review transcripts regularly for wording that drifts toward advice.
- Re-approve the wording when regulations or your services change.
P: Governance is what turns a chatbot from a risk into a controlled communication channel.
H2[vulnerable-clients-and-sensitive-moments]: Vulnerable clients and sensitive moments
P: Some visitors are recently bereaved, in financial distress or facing a big decision. The assistant should respond with care, avoid pressure and offer a person. Do not use urgency or fear to push a booking. If someone appears to be in crisis, provide appropriate support information rather than a sales flow.
H2[a-sample-conversation]: A sample conversation
TABLE:
H: Speaker | Message
R: Visitor | I am 58 and want to retire in five years. What should I do with my pension?
R: Assistant | That is a big and important question. I am an AI assistant, so I cannot give advice or say what you should do, and this chat does not create an advisory relationship. One of our authorised advisers can talk it through with you in a free 30-minute introductory meeting. Would you like to see times this week? Please do not share account or financial details here.

H2[a-compliance-review-routine]: A compliance review routine
P: Treat the assistant like any other client communication. Before launch, have your compliance officer read every document the assistant uses and every instruction it follows, and test a list of tempting questions: 'Which fund should I buy?', 'Is this a good time to invest?', 'What return will I get?' After launch, review a sample of transcripts every week for the first month and monthly afterwards, looking for anything that sounds like advice, a promise or an unapproved statement. Record what you reviewed and what you changed. When rules or your services change, repeat the process. This routine is what allows a regulated firm to use automation with confidence.
TABLE:
H: When | Check
R: Before launch | Documents, instructions, disclosures and tempting-question tests approved
R: First month | Weekly transcript sample review
R: Ongoing | Monthly review and record of changes
R: On any rule or service change | Re-approval of wording
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Letting the assistant comment on markets or products.
- Omitting required disclosures.
- Collecting account or identity details in the chat.
- Failing to keep records of the assistant's instructions and transcripts.
- Using urgency to push bookings with vulnerable prospects.
H2[working-with-your-compliance-officer]: Working with your compliance officer
P: Bring compliance in as a partner, not a gatekeeper at the end. Show them the assistant working in the testing area, walk through the disclosure and the forbidden topics together, and ask what they would like changed. Compliance officers often help design better controls, such as a standard statement the assistant always uses, or a rule that certain words trigger a handover. Their involvement also gives your firm a documented approval trail.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: Can a financial advisor use a chatbot?
A: Yes, to explain services, capture enquiries and book meetings, provided it gives no advice, uses approved wording and complies with record-keeping rules.
Q: Can the chatbot recommend products?
A: No. Recommendations are advice and must come from authorised advisers.
Q: Do chat transcripts need to be kept?
A: Possibly. Ask your compliance officer what your regulator requires.
Q: What should the chat say about risk and returns?
A: That it does not predict or promise returns, with any required risk statements approved by compliance.
Q: What should it collect?
A: Name, contact method, area of interest and timeframe, with consent. Not account numbers or detailed finances.
Q: How do I keep it compliant?
A: Involve compliance from the start, test tempting questions and review transcripts regularly.
