P: A chat widget is a small piece of your website that visitors talk to, and if it looks or sounds foreign, it undermines the rest of the site. A bright default blue on a soft green brand, a generic 'AI Assistant' name and a stiff greeting all signal that this is somebody else's tool bolted on. The good news is that the elements which make a widget feel like yours are few and quick to set: the colour, the name, the welcome message, the starter questions and the position on the page. Get those right and the widget looks like part of the business. This guide covers each in practice: how to choose and test a colour so it looks right and remains readable, how to name the assistant honestly, how to write a welcome message and starter questions that invite a first message, where to put the launcher, what to check on mobile and what white-label options are for. In Crescelo you set these in the Widget and Styling area of the dashboard: assistant name, brand colour, position, welcome message and suggested starter questions, with branding options for eligible plans.

H2[choose-the-colour-carefully]: Choose the colour carefully
OL:
- Start from your brand's main colour, the one used for your primary buttons.
- Check that white text is readable on it. Text and background need enough contrast; the common accessibility target is a ratio of at least 4.5 to 1 for normal text.
- If your colour is pale or bright yellow, choose a darker shade for the widget so text stays legible.
- Look at the widget on your real pages, on a phone and in bright light.
- Do not use colour alone to convey meaning; keep clear labels.
TABLE:
H: Brand colour | Risk | Fix
R: Pale pastel | White text is hard to read | Use a darker tone of the same hue
R: Bright yellow or lime | Very low contrast with white | Use a dark colour for the header with light text, or pick a deeper shade
R: Very dark or black | Fine for contrast, may look heavy | Check it against your page background
R: Two-tone brand | Which to pick? | Use the colour that your main buttons use
P: A free contrast checker will tell you the ratio for any pair of colours. Two minutes of checking prevents the most common accessibility failure in widgets; see [accessibility and WCAG for chatbots](/blog/accessibility-wcag-compliance-website-chatbots).
H2[name-the-assistant-honestly]: Name the assistant honestly
UL:
- Visitors should know they are talking to an AI. A name such as 'Riverside Assistant' or 'Ask Riverside' is friendly and honest.
- Avoid human names and photos that suggest a real person is typing, unless a person actually takes over; some places require disclosure that a chatbot is automated.
- Keep it short so it fits on a phone.
- Use your business's name so visitors recognise it.
P: If a human takes over the chat, say so clearly in the conversation. See [when and how to hand over](/blog/live-human-handover-flow-design).
H2[write-a-welcome-message-that-starts-conversations]: Write a welcome message that starts conversations
TABLE:
H: Weak | Better
R: Hi! How can I help you today? | Hi, I am Riverside's assistant. Ask me about prices, opening hours or booking a visit.
R: Welcome to our website. | Hello! Looking for a time this week? I can show what is free.
R: Chat with us! | Questions about your first appointment? Ask away.
P: The best welcome messages name two or three things the assistant can do, in the words customers use. Keep it to one or two sentences. See [writing chat welcome messages](/blog/chatbot-welcome-message-copywriting).
H2[starter-questions-are-your-most-useful-pixels]: Starter questions are your most useful pixels
UL:
- Use the three or four questions customers ask most; your unanswered and popular question lists will tell you.
- Word them as customers would: 'How much is a haircut?' rather than 'Pricing information'.
- Include one action, such as 'Book an appointment'.
- Keep each short enough to fit on one line on a phone.
- Review them each quarter as demand changes.
P: Starter questions lower the effort of the first message. On a phone, tapping is far easier than typing, so they often raise the share of visitors who start a conversation.
H2[choose-the-position]: Choose the position
TABLE:
H: Position | Considerations
R: Bottom right | The convention; visitors expect it
R: Bottom left | Useful if the right side has a cookie banner, a back-to-top button or another widget
R: Elsewhere | Rarely a good idea
P: Check pages with sticky footers, cookie banners and forms so the launcher never covers a submit button. On mobile, the launcher should not overlap your call or booking buttons.

TABLE:
H: Check | What good looks like

H2[keep-the-voice-consistent]: Keep the voice consistent
P: The look is only half of the brand; the assistant's replies must sound like you too. Set the tone in the Behavior and Rules area, and see [making the assistant sound like your business](/blog/brand-voice-alignment-ai-receptionist).
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Leaving the default name and greeting.
- Choosing a colour without checking contrast.
- Human names and photos that mislead.
- A welcome message that offers nothing specific.
- Covering important buttons.
- Not checking mobile.
H2[a-quick-branding-checklist]: A quick branding checklist
UL:
- Brand colour set and checked for contrast.
- Assistant name short, honest and recognisable.
- Welcome message names two or three things the assistant can do.
- Three or four starter questions, worded as customers ask them.
- Launcher position clear of banners and buttons.
- Tested on a phone and with the keyboard.
P: Run through the list whenever you redesign your site, since a new theme can change what the widget sits on top of.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: What can I customise in the Crescelo widget?
A: The assistant name, brand colour, position, welcome message and starter questions, plus branding options on eligible plans.
Q: How do I choose a colour that is readable?
A: Start from your brand colour, check that text has at least a 4.5 to 1 contrast ratio and darken the shade if needed.
Q: Should I give the assistant a human name?
A: Prefer an honest name that shows it is an assistant. Avoid suggesting a person is typing unless one is.
Q: Where should the widget sit?
A: Bottom right by convention, or bottom left if something else occupies that corner. Make sure it never covers key buttons.
Q: What makes a good welcome message?
A: One or two sentences naming the things the assistant can help with, in customers' words.
Q: What is white-label?
A: Replacing the platform's branding with your own on plans that allow it.
