P: Before you pay for an AI website assistant, it makes sense to ask whether it will pay for itself. The honest answer is that it depends on your numbers: how many visitors and enquiries you get, how many you currently lose, what a booking is worth and what the assistant costs. This guide gives you a straightforward method and formulas to work it out with your own figures, worked examples for three types of business and a way to check the result after a month, so you are judging real outcomes and not promises.
NOTE: The figures in this article are illustrations to show how the method works, not forecasts or benchmarks. Use your own numbers, and be cautious in your assumptions.
H2[the-simple-version-one-formula]: The simple version: one formula
P: Monthly return = (extra bookings or sales recovered × average value per booking) + (staff time saved × hourly cost) − monthly cost of the assistant.
P: If the result is positive, it pays for itself. To turn it into a ratio, divide the benefit by the cost. The work is in estimating the two benefit terms honestly.
H2[step-1-find-your-baseline]: Step 1: Find your baseline
OL:
- **Website visitors per month**, from your analytics.
- **Enquiries per month** by every route: forms, calls, messages, emails.
- **Bookings per month** that come from the website.
- **Enquiries you cannot answer in time:** out-of-hours messages, missed calls, forms answered a day later.
- **Average value of a booking** (revenue or gross profit; use the number you trust).
- **Time your team spends on repetitive questions** each week.
P: If you do not know some of these, estimate cautiously and mark them as estimates. You can improve them by tracking for a few weeks.
H2[step-2-estimate-the-recovered-enquiries]: Step 2: Estimate the recovered enquiries
P: The assistant helps most where enquiries are currently lost. Think in three buckets.
TABLE:
H: Bucket | How it helps | How to estimate
R: Out-of-hours enquiries | Answers and books at once | Number arriving outside working hours × share that would book if answered
R: Busy-hour enquiries | Answers while your team is occupied | Number missed or delayed × share that would book
R: Visitors who leave with a question | Answers the question that stopped them | Visitors who reach your pricing or contact page and leave × a small share
P: Use conservative conversion rates. If you are not sure, assume that only 10 percent of extra conversations become bookings. You can adjust upward once you have real data.
H2[step-3-estimate-the-staff-time-saved]: Step 3: Estimate the staff time saved
P: If your team answers the same twenty questions repeatedly, count the minutes. Ten questions a day at three minutes each is 30 minutes a day, or around 10 hours a month. If the assistant handles most, multiply the hours saved by the hourly cost of the person who would have answered. Be realistic: some questions still need a person, and you will spend time reviewing the assistant.

H2[what-can-make-the-return-smaller]: What can make the return smaller
UL:
- Very low traffic, so few enquiries are lost to begin with.
- A low-value booking with thin margins.
- An assistant with poor content that answers badly or too rarely.
- No one acting on leads that the assistant captures.
- Setup that is never tested, so bookings fail or confirmations do not arrive.
P: The strongest lever is your own follow-through: read the conversations, fix gaps and respond to leads quickly.
H2[step-5-check-the-result-after-a-month]: Step 5: Check the result after a month
OL:
- Record bookings and leads from the chat.
- Tag those that came out of hours or during busy periods; these are the clearest gains.
- Ask new customers how they found you and whether the chat helped.
- Compare with the same month before, and with seasonality in mind.
- Read a sample of conversations to see missed opportunities.
- Recalculate with real numbers.
P: If the result is below your estimate, look first at content and follow-up before blaming the tool. See [how to review your chatbot's conversations each week](/blog/audit-website-chatbot-conversations) and [cost per booked appointment](/blog/cost-per-booked-appointment-calculation).
H2[beyond-the-direct-numbers]: Beyond the direct numbers
UL:
- **Better data:** you learn what customers ask and where your website fails to answer.
- **Consistency:** the same accurate answers at every hour.
- **Team morale:** fewer interruptions.
- **Speed to lead:** faster first response, which affects conversion; see [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-service-businesses).
- **Resilience:** coverage during holidays and peaks.
P: These are harder to price, so do not count them in your first calculation, but they are real.

P: Copy this into a spreadsheet and fill in your own numbers. Each row has a formula in the third column.
TABLE:
H: Line | Your number | How to work it out
R: A. Enquiries lost or delayed per month |  | Out-of-hours plus busy-hour plus abandoned visitors
R: B. Share that would book if answered at once |  | Start with 10 to 15 percent and adjust with data
R: C. Extra bookings per month |  | A × B
R: D. Average value per booking |  | Revenue or gross profit, whichever you trust
R: E. Extra revenue per month |  | C × D
R: F. Hours saved per month |  | Repetitive questions per week × minutes each × 4 ÷ 60
R: G. Hourly cost of your time or staff |  | Wage plus overhead
R: H. Staff time value |  | F × G
R: I. Monthly cost |  | Platform plus AI usage plus review time
R: J. Net monthly return |  | E + H − I
R: K. Return ratio |  | (E + H) ÷ I
P: If K is above 1, the assistant covers its cost on your assumptions. Test how sensitive the answer is by halving B. If the result is still positive, you have a comfortable margin; if it turns negative, you need more evidence before buying.
H2[break-even-how-many-bookings-do-you-need]: Break-even: how many bookings do you need?
P: Break-even is the number of extra bookings per month that covers the cost. Divide your monthly cost by the value of a booking. If the total cost is $165 and a booking is worth $70, you need about 2.4 extra bookings, so three. If a booking is worth $600, you need a fraction of one. This is the most useful number to write down before buying, because it turns a fuzzy question into a specific target that you can check after a month.
TABLE:
H: Monthly cost | Value per booking | Bookings needed to break even
R: $60 | $50 | 2
R: $60 | $300 | 1
R: $165 | $70 | 3
R: $165 | $450 | 1
R: $430 | $120 | 4
R: $430 | $800 | 1
H2[traps-in-roi-calculations]: Traps in ROI calculations
UL:
- **Counting all chats as new business.** Many chats come from people who would have booked anyway. Count only the extra ones.
- **Using revenue rather than margin** when your margin is thin.
- **Ignoring the cost of your time** to set up and review.
- **Forgetting seasonality,** which can make one month look better or worse than it is.
- **Attributing everything to the assistant** when other changes happened at the same time.
- **Over-precision.** A range is more honest than a single number.
P: A sensible way to present the result to a partner or team is a range: pessimistic, expected and optimistic, each with its assumptions.
H2[deciding-whether-to-try-it]: Deciding whether to try it
P: Because the assistant has a low setup cost and monthly plans, the risk of trying it is small compared with the potential return in many businesses. A reasonable approach is to run it for one to three months, measure against the baseline you wrote down, and keep it if it beats the break-even. H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: How do I calculate the ROI of an AI chatbot?
A: Estimate the extra bookings or sales it recovers multiplied by their value, add staff time saved, subtract the monthly cost, and compare the benefit with the cost.
Q: What conversion rate should I assume?
A: Be conservative. Assume a small share of recovered enquiries become bookings, then replace the assumption with real data after a month.
Q: How quickly does an AI assistant pay back?
A: It depends on your traffic and booking value. Businesses with high-value bookings and many out-of-hours enquiries can see payback quickly; low-traffic sites take longer or may not benefit.
Q: Should I count staff time saved?
A: Yes, but carefully. Count only the time that is genuinely freed, and remember you will spend some time reviewing the assistant.
Q: What costs should I include?
A: The platform subscription, AI usage, the time to set up and a few minutes a week for review.
Q: What if the numbers do not work?
A: Do not buy it yet. Track your enquiries and lost opportunities for a month, then recalculate.
