P: The first message your chatbot shows decides whether most visitors ever start a conversation. A vague 'Hi, how can I help?' is easy to ignore. A specific, friendly line that tells the visitor what the assistant can do for them, and makes replying effortless, gets more responses. This guide explains what a good welcome message does, the principles of writing one, how to match the message to the page and to the time of day, how to add quick replies, and gives thirty examples for service businesses that you can adapt. It ends with a way to test which message works best for you.
H2[what-a-welcome-message-has-to-do]: What a welcome message has to do
OL:
- **Say what the assistant can do.** Visitors do not know if it can book, quote or answer questions.
- **Sound human and on brand.** Warm, plain and specific.
- **Make replying easy.** Give a low-effort way to start, such as a question or buttons.
- **Set honest expectations.** Say it is an AI assistant, and that a person is available if needed.
- **Respect the visitor's attention.** Short, not pushy.
P: If the message does these five things, it will beat most generic greetings.
H2[weak-versus-strong]: Weak versus strong
TABLE:
H: Weak | Why it fails | Stronger
R: Hi! How can I help you today? | Vague; gives no reason to reply | Hi, I am the Brook Salon assistant. Ask me about prices or book a time in one minute.
R: Welcome to our website! | States the obvious | Looking for a price or a free time? I can check both.
R: Chat with us | A command, not an offer | Questions before you book? I can answer them now.
R: We are online! | Untrue at 2 a.m. and unhelpful | I am an AI assistant and I am here any time. A real person is available in office hours.
H2[principles-of-a-good-greeting]: Principles of a good greeting
UL:
- **Lead with a benefit:** prices, times, quotes, answers.
- **Be specific to your business:** name it, name the top job.
- **Keep it under 25 words** for the first bubble.
- **Ask one easy question** or offer two or three buttons.
- **Use the visitor's words,** not your internal jargon.
- **Avoid over-promising:** do not claim instant everything.
- **Say it is an AI** clearly but briefly.
- **Match the tone of your brand:** playful for a barbershop, calm for a clinic.
H2[match-the-message-to-the-page]: Match the message to the page
TABLE:
H: Page | Visitor mindset | Opening message
R: Home | Exploring | Hi, I can tell you what we do, what it costs and find you a time. What would you like to know?
R: Services | Comparing options | Not sure which service suits you? Tell me what you need and I will suggest one.
R: Pricing | Worried about cost | Questions about prices or what is included? I can explain and show you the next available time.
R: Contact | Ready to reach out | Skip the form: ask me here and I can book you in or take a message.
R: Blog article | Learning | Questions about this topic or how it applies to you? Ask away.
R: Booking page | Ready to book | Need help picking a time? I can show you what is free this week.
P: Different messages on different pages raise relevance. If your product supports per-page greetings, use them on your three most visited pages first.
H2[match-the-message-to-the-time]: Match the message to the time
TABLE:
H: When | Message idea
R: Open hours | Ask me anything, or I can find you a time today. A team member is also on hand.
R: Evening or night | The team has gone home, but I can answer questions and book you in. Ask away.
R: Weekend | Open Saturday 9 to 2. I can book you in for then or answer questions now.
R: Holiday | We are closed until 4 January. I can answer questions and book your first appointment back.
H2[add-quick-replies]: Add quick replies
P: Buttons lower the effort of starting. Offer two to four options that reflect real visitor goals.
UL:
- 'See prices'
- 'Book a time'
- 'Ask a question'
- 'Talk to a person'
P: Quick replies also steer visitors toward things the assistant does well. Remove buttons nobody clicks and add the ones that match common conversations from your review. See [micro-copy for quick-reply buttons](/blog/micro-copy-quick-reply-buttons-conversational-prompts).
H2[thirty-examples-to-adapt]: Thirty examples to adapt
H3: Salons and barbershops
UL:
- Hi, I am the Brook Barbers assistant. Ask about prices or grab a slot in one minute.
- Need a trim before the weekend? I can show you the free times.
- Not sure what style suits you? Tell me about your hair and I will suggest a service.
- Bringing your child? I can tell you how long a kids' cut takes and book both of you.
- Booked out online? Ask me for the next opening and I will check.
H3: Clinics and dental
UL:
- Hello, I am the practice assistant. I can answer questions about new patients, prices and opening hours, and book you a visit.
- Nervous about your first visit? Ask me anything, and I will explain what happens.
- Looking for a check-up? I can show you the next free appointment.
- Not an emergency line: if you need urgent help, tell me and I will alert the team.
- Insurance questions? I can share the providers we work with.
H3: Home services
UL:
- Something broken? Tell me what happened and where, and I will get the right person to call you.
- Need a quote? Describe the job and I will book an inspection.
- Emergency? Tell me now, and I will alert the on-call engineer.
- Wondering if we cover your area? Give me your postcode.
- Not sure what it might cost? I can give you a ballpark and next steps.
H3: Real estate
UL:
- Interested in a property? Tell me which one and I can share details or book a viewing.
- Thinking of selling? I can explain how we work and book a valuation.
- Searching by area? Tell me what you are after and I will show what fits.
- Want a viewing this weekend? I can check the times.
H3: Professional services
UL:
- Have a question about our services or how we work? Ask me, and I can book an initial call.
- I cannot give advice, but I can explain how we help and arrange a consultation.
- Not sure which service you need? Tell me the situation in a line, no details, and I will point you the right way.
- Want to book a first call? I can show you the times.
H3: Studios and classes
UL:
- Try a class? I can show times and book you a spot.
- Questions about memberships or drop-in prices? Ask me.
- First time here? I can tell you what to bring and what to expect.
H3: After hours and general
UL:
- Our team is offline, but I can help. Ask a question or book a time.
- Ask me anything about our services. If I do not know, I will say so and pass you to the team.
- I am an AI assistant. I can answer questions and book appointments, and a person is available in office hours.
H2[words-to-avoid]: Words to avoid
UL:
- Corporate jargon such as 'leverage' or 'solutions'.
- Over-excitement, exclamation marks everywhere and emoji floods.
- Claims you cannot support: 'instant answers to everything'.
- Anything that sounds like a pop-up ad.
- Pretending to be human.
H2[how-to-test-which-message-wins]: How to test which message wins
OL:
- Choose your current message as the control.
- Write one alternative that changes one thing, such as specificity or the first question.
- Run each for two weeks, or use an A/B feature if available, on similar traffic.
- Compare the share of visitors who reply and the share who book.
- Keep the winner and try another change.
TABLE:
H: Metric | Why it matters
R: Chat open rate | Whether the greeting attracts attention
R: Reply rate | Whether the first message invites a response
R: Bookings per 100 visitors | The outcome that counts
P: See [A/B testing chatbot starter prompts](/blog/ab-test-chatbot-starter-prompts) for a fuller method. Avoid testing many things at once.
H2[voice-and-tone-worksheet]: Voice and tone worksheet
P: Before you write, decide three things: how formal you are, how much humour you allow and what you never say. Write three adjectives for your brand, for example warm, plain and direct, and check each greeting against them. Then read it aloud. If you would not say it to a customer standing at your counter, rewrite it.

H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: What should a chatbot welcome message say?
A: What the assistant can do, in a friendly and specific way, with an easy first step such as a question or buttons.
Q: How long should it be?
A: Under about 25 words for the first bubble, with one clear prompt.
Q: Should the chatbot say it is an AI?
A: Yes. Briefly and clearly. It builds trust and often is a legal expectation.
Q: Should the greeting change by page?
A: Yes if you can. A message that matches what the visitor is looking at is more relevant.
Q: Should the chat open automatically?
A: Usually let visitors choose. If you use auto-open, do it after a delay and only once, and avoid stealing focus.
Q: How do I know which message is best?
A: Test one change at a time and compare reply and booking rates.
