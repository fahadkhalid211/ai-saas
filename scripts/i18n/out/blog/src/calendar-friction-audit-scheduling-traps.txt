P: You have set up online booking. Visitors are coming to your site. Yet fewer of them book than you expected. Before assuming demand is the issue, walk through the process as a first-time customer on a phone. Booking flows leak in small, unglamorous ways: a button that is hard to find, a form that asks too much, a calendar that shows no times, a confirmation that does not arrive. Each leak loses a few percent of visitors, and together they can lose most of them. This guide describes seven of the most common traps, shows how to test for each one in ten minutes, and suggests fixes. It is written for owners of salons, clinics, studios, trades and consultancies who use any scheduling tool, including Crescelo's built-in booking, Calendly or Cal.com. Where it mentions what Crescelo supports, it sticks to the facts: one set of opening hours, one slot length, a locked slot while booking, and a confirmation email with a private change link.
H2[how-to-run-the-audit]: How to run the audit
OL:
- Use a phone on mobile data, not your office computer.
- Start from your home page as if you had never seen the business.
- Try to book your most popular service, timing yourself.
- Write down every moment you hesitated, tapped the wrong thing or felt unsure.
- Repeat with someone who has never used your site.
P: Ten minutes of this reveals more than a month of statistics.
H2[trap-1-nobody-can-find-the-booking-button]: Trap 1: Nobody can find the booking button
TABLE:
H: Symptom | Test | Fix
R: The button sits below the fold or only in the menu | Can you find it in three seconds on a phone? | Put a clear 'Book' button at the top of the home and service pages
R: It has a vague label such as 'Contact' | Would a stranger know what happens next? | Use 'Book an appointment'
R: It is hidden in a footer | Scroll from the top | Repeat the button at natural decision points
P: A chat assistant helps here: it can offer to book right after answering a question, at the moment the visitor is ready.
H2[trap-2-too-many-steps-before-seeing-times]: Trap 2: Too many steps before seeing times
P: Every screen before the calendar loses people. Asking for an account, a long form, a service quiz and a phone number before showing availability tells the visitor that booking will be a chore. Show available times first and collect details afterwards. Ask only for what you need to serve them: name, contact detail and the service.
H2[trap-3-the-calendar-shows-no-availability]: Trap 3: The calendar shows no availability
UL:
- Opening hours set too narrowly, so few slots appear.
- A slot length that does not match how long you really need, leaving odd gaps.
- Dates far in the future showing nothing because of a booking window.
- Timezone confusion when visitors travel or book for someone elsewhere.
P: Open the calendar as a visitor and see how many days show times. If it looks empty, review your hours and slot length. Remember that Crescelo's built-in picker uses one set of opening hours and one slot length, and it hides slots already taken. It does not read your personal calendar, so a busy morning in Google Calendar will not block slots unless you close those hours in the settings. If you need different lengths per service or buffers between appointments, a tool such as Calendly or Cal.com, which Crescelo can link to, may fit better.
H2[trap-4-confusing-or-incomplete-slots]: Trap 4: Confusing or incomplete slots
TABLE:
H: Problem | Effect | Fix
R: Times in an unfamiliar format | Wrong bookings | Show a clear date, weekday and time
R: No indication of duration | Client unsure if it fits | State how long it takes
R: A slot disappears when clicked | Frustration | Lock the slot while the visitor completes the form, as Crescelo does
R: Too many options at once | Decision fatigue | Offer the next few available times first
H2[trap-5-the-mobile-experience-is-poor]: Trap 5: The mobile experience is poor
P: Most bookings are made on phones. Test the flow with one hand. Tap targets should be large enough, forms should use the right keyboard for email and phone fields, and the page should load in a couple of seconds on mobile data. A chat widget should not cover the buttons of the booking form; see [performance of embedded chat widgets](/blog/performance-optimization-embedded-chat-widgets).
H2[trap-6-no-reassurance-about-what-happens-next]: Trap 6: No reassurance about what happens next
UL:
- A visitor who taps 'Book' and sees nothing may book twice or leave.
- Show a clear confirmation screen with the date, time and service.
- Send an email confirmation immediately, with a link to change or cancel and an add-to-calendar option.
- Explain what to bring or do, and how to reach you.
P: Crescelo sends a confirmation email with a private change link and offers add-to-calendar options in the chat. Test that the email arrives quickly and is not in spam; see [making sure booking emails reach the inbox](/blog/configure-custom-smtp-resend-booking-emails).
H2[trap-7-hidden-rules-and-surprise-conditions]: Trap 7: Hidden rules and surprise conditions
P: Nothing loses trust faster than a surprise at the end: a cancellation fee, a required deposit, a service you do not offer at that location. State conditions before the visitor commits, in a sentence, not a legal block. If a service needs a consultation first, say so on the service page and in the assistant's answers.
H2[scoring-your-audit]: Scoring your audit
TABLE:
H: Trap | Pass | Fail
R: 1. Findability | Button visible in three seconds | Hidden or vaguely labelled
R: 2. Steps | Times shown before details | Account or long form first
R: 3. Availability | Times shown for the coming weeks | Empty or nearly empty calendar
R: 4. Slot clarity | Date, time and duration obvious | Ambiguous
R: 5. Mobile | Easy one-handed | Awkward or slow
R: 6. Reassurance | Confirmation on screen and email | Nothing happens visibly
R: 7. Rules | Conditions stated before booking | Surprises at the end
P: Fix the failures in order. The first two usually have the biggest effect.
H2[after-the-fix-measure]: After the fix: measure
UL:
- Count visits to the booking page against completed bookings.
- Watch where chat conversations about booking end.
- Ask new customers whether anything was confusing.
- Re-run the audit every quarter and after any website change.
H2[a-quick-fix-list-for-this-week]: A quick fix list for this week
UL:
- Move the booking button to the top of your home and service pages.
- Widen your opening hours by an hour on your busiest day and see if bookings rise.
- Check that the confirmation email arrives within a minute and includes the location.
- Book once yourself on a phone and delete the test booking.
- Add one sentence about your cancellation policy to the booking page.
P: Small changes like these are cheap to try and easy to undo, which makes them a good way to learn what your visitors respond to before larger changes.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: Why do visitors abandon online booking?
A: Usually friction: a hard-to-find button, too many steps, an empty calendar, poor mobile experience or unclear conditions.
Q: How do I test my booking flow?
A: Book your most popular service on a phone as if you were a stranger, time yourself and note every hesitation.
Q: Does Crescelo's booking support different lengths per service?
A: No. It uses one set of opening hours and one slot length. For per-service durations, link to a scheduling tool such as Calendly or Cal.com.
Q: Will the chat assistant help people book?
A: Yes. It can offer real available times in the chat when a visitor is ready, and send a confirmation email.
Q: How often should I audit?
A: Every quarter and after any change to the site or booking settings.
Q: What is the most common trap?
A: Making visitors work before showing them available times.
