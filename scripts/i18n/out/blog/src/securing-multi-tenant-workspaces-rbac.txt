P: Your chat dashboard holds things with different levels of sensitivity. Conversations contain customers' words and contact details. Settings control what your assistant says. Billing pages hold your plan and invoices. AI provider keys can spend money. Not everyone on your team needs to see or change all of it. The receptionist who replies to visitors does not need to change your billing plan; the accountant who downloads invoices does not need to read customer conversations; a contractor updating your knowledge base should not be able to delete the whole workspace. Role-based access control, or RBAC, is the practice of giving each person a role that grants exactly the access they need. This guide explains the idea, describes the roles typically used in a workspace such as Crescelo's (owner, admin, billing manager, support agent and viewer), gives a sample matrix, shows how to assign roles using least privilege, how to handle joiners and leavers, how to use an audit trail and how to review access regularly. Check the exact permissions in your own dashboard, since they can be refined over time.
H2[why-roles-matter]: Why roles matter
UL:
- **Privacy:** fewer people see customers' conversations.
- **Safety:** fewer people can make destructive or costly changes by mistake.
- **Accountability:** named accounts with roles show who did what.
- **Compliance:** data protection rules expect access limited to those who need it.
- **Resilience:** if one account is compromised, the damage is limited to what that role can do.
H2[the-roles]: The roles
TABLE:
H: Role | Typical purpose | Typical access
R: Owner | The business owner or account holder | Everything, including billing and team management
R: Admin | Someone who manages the assistant day to day | Assistants, knowledge, settings, leads and conversations; not usually ownership
R: Billing manager | Finance or an office manager | Plans, invoices and payment details; no need for conversations
R: Support agent | Front desk or customer service | Conversations, leads, bookings and handovers; not settings or keys
R: Viewer | A manager or a client who needs to look, not touch | Read-only access to selected areas
P: In Crescelo's workspace, owners and admins have full access, and other roles are limited to the areas that suit them, for example billing pages for billing managers. Some actions require a specific role, and master administration of the platform is a separate, protected role held by the platform operator.
H2[a-sample-role-matrix]: A sample role matrix
TABLE:
H: Area | Owner | Admin | Billing manager | Support agent | Viewer
R: Conversations and leads | Yes | Yes | No | Yes | Read
R: Bookings | Yes | Yes | No | Yes | Read
R: Knowledge base | Yes | Yes | No | Limited | Read
R: Assistant settings and instructions | Yes | Yes | No | No | No
R: AI provider keys | Yes | Yes | No | No | No
R: Team members and roles | Yes | Limited | No | No | No
R: Billing and plan | Yes | No | Yes | No | No
R: Delete workspace or data | Yes | No | No | No | No
P: This is a suggested pattern for planning, not a statement of exact permissions. Confirm what each role can do in your dashboard before relying on it, and test by signing in as each role.
H2[apply-least-privilege]: Apply least privilege
OL:
- Start every new person at the lowest role that lets them do their job.
- Raise access only when there is a specific need, and lower it when the need ends.
- Keep owners to one or two people.
- Give contractors the narrowest role and remove it when the project finishes.
- Prefer separate accounts over shared logins; never share a password.
- Review the sensitive areas separately: provider keys, billing and exports.
H2[joiners-movers-and-leavers]: Joiners, movers and leavers
TABLE:
H: Event | Action
R: Someone joins | Invite with a named account and the smallest suitable role; turn on two-factor sign-in
R: Someone changes job | Change the role the same day
R: A contractor finishes | Remove access immediately
R: An employee leaves | Remove the account, rotate any shared secrets and review the audit trail
R: The owner changes | Transfer ownership deliberately, and remove the previous owner
P: In Crescelo, sessions are checked against the account's current role and workspace on each request, so demoting or removing a member takes effect immediately, not when their session expires.
H2[add-two-factor-authentication]: Add two-factor authentication
P: Roles limit what an account can do; two-factor sign-in reduces the chance of someone else using it. Prioritise owners and admins, since their accounts can change everything. See [securing your chatbot account](/blog/setup-rbac-2fa-team-security) for setup steps.
H2[use-the-audit-trail]: Use the audit trail
UL:
- Review the log of important actions monthly: changes to settings, sources, keys and members.
- After any incident, check who did what and when.
- Investigate unexpected changes promptly, and ask the person who made them.
- Use it to improve roles: if a support agent keeps needing an admin action, decide whether they need that access or a process change.
H2[review-access-on-a-schedule]: Review access on a schedule
TABLE:
H: Frequency | Check
R: Monthly | Audit trail for unusual changes
R: Quarterly | The list of members and roles: remove anyone who no longer needs access
R: Twice a year | API keys and shared secrets: rotate as needed
R: After staff changes | Immediate review of the affected accounts
P: Put the review in the calendar, since access tends to accumulate silently. Link it to your regular [chat analytics review](/blog/service-business-chat-analytics-playbook).
H2[data-isolation-is-the-other-half]: Data isolation is the other half
P: Roles decide what a person can do inside a workspace. Isolation decides that nobody outside it can see in. A trustworthy platform does both; see [multi-tenant data isolation](/blog/multi-tenant-saas-chatbot-architecture) for the platform side.
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Making everyone an owner.
- Sharing one login.
- Leaving contractor access in place.
- Never reviewing roles.
- Giving support staff access to keys and billing.
- Assuming roles replace two-factor sign-in.
H2[a-worked-example]: A worked example
P: A dental practice has six people using the dashboard: the practice owner, an office manager, two receptionists, a part-time marketing freelancer and the practice's accountant. The owner keeps the owner role and turns on two-factor sign-in. The office manager becomes an admin, because they maintain the assistant's documents. The receptionists get the support agent role, so they can read conversations and take handovers but cannot change instructions or see keys. The freelancer is a viewer for the month of a campaign and is removed afterwards. The accountant is a billing manager and never sees a conversation. Each person can do their job, and nobody can reach anything they do not need.
TABLE:
H: Person | Role | Why
R: Practice owner | Owner | Ultimate responsibility, billing and team
R: Office manager | Admin | Maintains documents and settings
R: Receptionists | Support agent | Handle conversations and bookings
R: Freelancer | Viewer, temporary | Needs to see, not change; removed after the campaign
R: Accountant | Billing manager | Invoices only
H2[handle-exceptions-deliberately]: Handle exceptions deliberately
P: Sometimes a person needs more access than their role allows for a specific task, such as a receptionist who must update opening hours before a holiday. Rather than promoting them permanently, change the setting yourself or grant a higher role temporarily and record when to remove it. Put the removal in the calendar. Temporary access that is never withdrawn is the most common way roles drift away from least privilege over time.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: What is role-based access control?
A: Giving each team member a role that grants only the access their job needs, such as owner, admin, billing manager, support agent or viewer.
Q: Which roles does Crescelo have?
A: Owner, admin, billing manager, support agent and viewer. Check the dashboard for the exact permissions of each.
Q: Who should be an owner?
A: One or two trusted people, since owners have full control including billing and team management.
Q: What happens when I remove someone?
A: Their access ends immediately, because sessions are checked against the account's current state on every request.
Q: How often should I review roles?
A: Quarterly, and immediately after staff changes.
Q: Do roles replace two-factor authentication?
A: No. Roles limit what an account can do; two-factor sign-in protects the account itself.
