P: Reviews are among the strongest reasons a stranger chooses you. A business with sixty recent, specific reviews looks safer than one with three, and search results and maps reward review volume and freshness. Yet most satisfied customers never leave a review, not because they are unhappy but because nobody asked, or the process was too much bother. Asking is the single biggest lever, and doing it well is a matter of timing, wording and making it easy. This guide explains when to ask, how to word the request, which channels work, how to make the review link a one-tap action, which rules platforms and consumer law impose, how to handle negative feedback gracefully and how a chat assistant helps without pretending to do more than it does. Crescelo's assistant answers questions and books appointments; it does not send review requests on its own, so the requests here are sent from your own email or messaging tools, and the assistant helps by pointing people to reviews and answering questions about them.
H2[why-customers-do-not-review]: Why customers do not review
TABLE:
H: Reason | What helps
R: Nobody asked | Ask every customer
R: It felt like effort | Send a direct link that opens the review form
R: They forgot | One polite reminder
R: They were unsure what to write | Offer a one-line prompt
R: They were unhappy and unheard | Give them a way to tell you privately first
H2[when-to-ask]: When to ask
UL:
- **After the result is visible:** a haircut can be reviewed the same day; a garden design after the plants have grown; a dental check-up when the visit is fresh.
- **At a moment of satisfaction:** just after a compliment, a completed job or a rebooking.
- **Not during a problem:** if there is an open complaint, resolve it first.
- **Not too late:** after a few weeks the memory fades.
P: A good default is the same day or the next day for quick services, and within a few days for jobs that need time to show results.
H2[how-to-word-the-request]: How to word the request
TABLE:
H: Element | Example
R: Personal thanks | Thanks for choosing us, Sam.
R: The ask | If you were happy with your visit, would you mind sharing a review?
R: Why it matters | It helps other people in the neighbourhood find us.
R: The link | One tap: [direct review link]
R: A prompt | A line about what you liked, such as the service, the friendliness or the result, is plenty.
R: No pressure | No worries if you do not have time.
P: Keep it short and warm, and make sure the link works on a phone. Do not script the review or tell customers what to say.
H2[channels]: Channels
UL:
- **Email** after the appointment, from your own email tool.
- **A message** from your business number or a messaging app, if the customer has agreed to be contacted that way.
- **In person**, with a printed card or a QR code at the counter.
- **On the receipt or thank-you page**, if you have one.
- **On your website**, where the assistant can point to your review profile when visitors ask about reputation.
P: Spread the asks across channels but do not repeat the same request to one person more than once or twice.
H2[make-the-link-a-one-tap-action]: Make the link a one-tap action
OL:
- Find your review profile on the platform you want reviews on, for example your Google Business Profile.
- Copy the direct link that opens the review box, not the general profile page.
- Shorten it if needed, and test it on a phone.
- Put it in your templates so nobody has to hunt for it.
- Add a QR code to printed materials.
H2[follow-the-rules]: Follow the rules
UL:
- Review platforms generally forbid paying for reviews or offering rewards for positive ones; do not do it.
- Do not only ask customers you think are happy. Selective asking, known as review gating, breaches the rules of some platforms and consumer protection law in some places.
- Do not write reviews yourself or have staff post them.
- Do not ask family and friends to pretend to be customers.
- Tell customers they are free to leave honest feedback of any kind.
- Consumer authorities in several countries actively pursue fake or manipulated reviews.
P: This is general information, not legal advice. Check your platform's guidelines and local rules.
H2[handle-negative-feedback-well]: Handle negative feedback well
OL:
- Thank the reviewer and respond promptly and calmly.
- Do not argue or reveal private details.
- Acknowledge the problem and offer to resolve it offline: give a contact route.
- Fix what can be fixed, and update the review response if it is resolved.
- Learn from the pattern: repeated complaints show what to improve.
TABLE:
H: Do | Do not
R: Apologise for a bad experience where it was one | Accuse the reviewer of lying
R: Keep it short and factual | Write an essay
R: Invite them to contact you | Argue in public
R: Thank them for the feedback | Delete or hide legitimate reviews where you have the choice to respond
P: Prospective customers read responses to bad reviews as closely as the reviews themselves.
H2[where-the-chat-assistant-fits]: Where the chat assistant fits
UL:
- It answers 'Do you have good reviews?' with facts from your documents, such as your rating and a link to your profile, if you have loaded them; see [using social proof in chat](/blog/social-proof-reviews-ai-chat).
- It helps visitors who want to leave feedback find the right link.
- It can capture a complaint as a lead for a person to follow up.
- It does not send scheduled review requests, so send those from your email or messaging tools, ideally as part of the follow-up routine in [after the appointment](/blog/post-appointment-nurture-rebooking-sequences).
P: Never let the assistant claim ratings or review counts that are not in your documents, and update them regularly.
H2[a-simple-monthly-routine]: A simple monthly routine
UL:
- Count new reviews and the average rating.
- Reply to every review.
- Note themes in praise and complaints.
- Check that the review link still works.
- Set a target for the next month, such as one review request per completed job.
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Asking only happy customers.
- Offering incentives.
- Sending a link to the general profile instead of the review box.
- Asking too early or too often.
- Ignoring negative reviews.
- Letting the assistant quote out-of-date ratings.
H2[templates-you-can-adapt]: Templates you can adapt
TABLE:
H: Moment | Message
R: Same day, after a visit | Hi Sam, thanks for coming in today. If you enjoyed your visit, a quick review would help others find us: [link]. Thank you!
R: A week later, one reminder | Hi Sam, just a gentle reminder in case you had a moment to share your experience: [link]. No worries if not.
R: To someone who had a problem | Hi Sam, we are sorry your visit was not what you hoped. We would like to put it right. Could you reply or call us on 0123 456 789?
P: The last template matters: a customer who is unhappy and can reach you directly is far more likely to give you a chance to fix things than to write a public complaint.
H2[use-reviews-inside-your-own-content]: Use reviews inside your own content
UL:
- Quote a few specific reviews, with permission, on your service pages.
- Ask the assistant's documents to include your rating and review count, with the date they were last updated.
- Share praise with the team; it helps morale.
- Turn frequent complaints into fixes.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: How do I ask customers for reviews without being pushy?
A: Ask once, shortly after a good experience, with a personal thanks, a direct link and no pressure. One polite reminder is enough.
Q: Can I offer a discount for a review?
A: No. Most platforms forbid incentives for reviews, and doing so can breach consumer law.
Q: Should I only ask happy customers?
A: No. Ask everyone; selective asking is against many platform rules and can be illegal.
Q: Can Crescelo send review requests?
A: No. It answers questions and books appointments; send requests from your own email or messaging tools.
Q: How should I respond to a bad review?
A: Thank them, apologise where warranted, offer to resolve it offline and never argue in public.
Q: How many reviews do I need?
A: There is no fixed number, but steady, recent, specific reviews matter more than a burst followed by silence.
