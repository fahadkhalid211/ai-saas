P: A chatbot that quietly does its job can be invisible to the people paying for it. The owner sees a monthly subscription and wonders what it delivers. A manager sees fewer phone calls but cannot say how many. An agency runs assistants for clients and needs to show they are worth renewing. A short monthly report solves all of these: one page that says what happened, what it was worth, what needs attention and what will change next month. It does not need charts of everything; the point is to make decisions easy. This guide gives you a simple structure for a one-page report, explains which numbers to include and which to skip, shows how to add honest commentary, how to handle cost and value, how to end with actions and how to present it to different audiences. It uses the kinds of information available from your Crescelo dashboard, such as conversations, leads, bookings and unanswered questions, and avoids inventing metrics the platform does not provide.
H2[principles-of-a-good-report]: Principles of a good report
UL:
- **One page.** If it does not fit, you are including too much.
- **Answers, not data.** Each number should support a point.
- **Compare with last month** so trends are visible.
- **Honest.** Include what did not work.
- **Ends with actions.** A report without decisions is a diary.
H2[the-template]: The template
TABLE:
H: Section | Content
R: 1. Headline | Two sentences: the main result and the main issue
R: 2. Key numbers | Five or six figures, with change from last month
R: 3. What we learned | Three to four short observations from conversations
R: 4. Cost and value | What it cost and what it produced, modestly stated
R: 5. Problems and risks | Anything that went wrong or needs attention
R: 6. Actions | What will change next month, and who owns it
H2[choose-the-key-numbers]: Choose the key numbers
TABLE:
H: Number | Why it belongs
R: Conversations | The size of the activity
R: Leads captured | The direct business output
R: Bookings made | The most tangible outcome
R: Questions answered without staff | Time saved, estimated with care
R: Unanswered questions | Quality and where to improve
R: Handovers to a person | Complexity and staff involvement
P: Use only figures you can get from your dashboard and conversations. Do not include vanity numbers such as total messages, which say little. If you cannot measure something, do not estimate it and present it as fact.
H2[add-commentary-that-a-reader-can-use]: Add commentary that a reader can use
P: Numbers need interpretation. For each key number, add a sentence saying what it means.
TABLE:
H: Number | Weak comment | Useful comment
R: Leads: 42 (up from 35) | Leads increased. | Leads rose by seven, mostly after we added prices to the services page.
R: Unanswered: 31 (down from 48) | Fewer unanswered. | We added answers on parking and children's appointments; the remaining gaps are mostly about wedding packages.
R: Handovers: 9 | Nine handovers. | Six were complaints or custom quotes, which is appropriate; three were routine and we have fixed the documents.
H2[cost-and-value-honestly]: Cost and value, honestly
UL:
- **Cost:** the subscription plus AI usage at your provider, if you bring your own key. Include a few minutes of staff time to maintain it.
- **Value:** bookings made through chat, multiplied by their typical value; leads, weighted by your normal conversion rate; staff time saved, only if the time was reallocated.
- **Caveats:** some of those customers might have booked anyway. Say so.
TABLE:
H: Item | Example
R: Monthly cost | $149 plan plus about $12 AI usage
R: Bookings from chat | 14
R: Average booking value | $70
R: Indicative value | About $980, before allowing for people who would have booked anyway
P: A cautious statement is more persuasive than an inflated one. See [chat analytics for service businesses](/blog/service-business-chat-analytics-playbook) for how to value outcomes.
H2[problems-and-risks]: Problems and risks
UL:
- Wrong answers found in the month, and how they were fixed.
- Outages or slow responses.
- Complaints about the assistant.
- Content that is out of date.
- Upcoming changes, such as a price change, that need the documents updated.
P: Reporting problems builds trust. A report with no problems either means nothing was checked or something is being hidden.
H2[actions-for-next-month]: Actions for next month
TABLE:
H: Action | Owner | Due
R: Add wedding package details to the knowledge base | Sam | Week 1
R: Test the new price list questions | Priya | Week 2
R: Review five handovers | Owner | Week 3
R: Update opening hours for the holiday | Sam | Before the 20th
P: Keep to three to five actions, each with a name and a date.
H2[tailor-it-to-the-reader]: Tailor it to the reader
TABLE:
H: Reader | Emphasise
R: Owner | Leads, bookings, cost and value
R: Front desk manager | Handovers, unanswered questions, workload
R: Client of an agency | Outcomes, improvements made, plan for next month
R: Marketing lead | Popular questions, sources of leads, content ideas
P: If you serve several readers, keep the core page the same and add a short section for each.
H2[make-it-a-routine]: Make it a routine
OL:
- Choose a fixed day, such as the first Monday.
- Gather the numbers in fifteen minutes from the dashboard.
- Read five conversations to write the commentary.
- Fill the template.
- Share it, and discuss the actions in a short meeting.
- Save the reports, so you can look back over the year.
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Reporting everything.
- Numbers without meaning.
- Overstating value.
- Hiding problems.
- No actions.
- Not comparing with the previous month.
H2[a-filled-in-example]: A filled-in example
TABLE:
H: Section | Example
R: Headline | Chat produced 42 leads and 14 bookings in March, up from 35 and 11. Wedding-package questions are the biggest gap.
R: Key numbers | Conversations 310 (+8%); leads 42 (+7); bookings 14 (+3); unanswered 31 (-17); handovers 9 (same)
R: What we learned | Visitors ask about parking, children and wedding packages; price questions convert best when a range is stated
R: Cost and value | Cost about $161; indicative booking value about $980, before allowing for people who would have booked anyway
R: Problems | One wrong answer about Saturday hours, fixed the same day; the opening hours document was out of date
R: Actions | Add wedding details; review five handovers; check hours before each holiday
P: Everything above fits on one page, can be read in two minutes and leads directly to decisions. Notice that it says what went wrong and what was done about it.
H2[store-and-compare-reports]: Store and compare reports
P: Keep every report in one folder. After six months, read them in order: you will see which changes helped, which numbers move with the seasons and whether the same problems keep returning. That history is worth more than any single month, and it makes renewal or budget conversations easy because you can show a trend, not just a snapshot.
H2[when-the-numbers-are-small]: When the numbers are small
P: A small business may have only a few dozen conversations a month. Do not let that discourage reporting: small numbers still show direction, and every conversation can be read. Focus on the qualitative sections, what visitors asked and what you changed, and avoid drawing firm conclusions from a swing of two or three leads. Say plainly when a change is within normal variation.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: What should a chatbot report include?
A: Key numbers such as conversations, leads, bookings, unanswered questions and handovers, plus commentary, cost and value, problems and actions.
Q: How long should it be?
A: One page. If it needs more, you are probably including too much.
Q: How do I calculate value?
A: Multiply bookings and likely leads by typical value, subtract cost and be cautious about attribution.
Q: Where do I get the numbers?
A: From the dashboard's conversations, leads and bookings, plus your own sampling of transcripts.
Q: Should I include bad news?
A: Yes. Problems and fixes build trust and show that the assistant is being managed.
Q: How often should I report?
A: Monthly, with a short weekly check on unanswered questions.
