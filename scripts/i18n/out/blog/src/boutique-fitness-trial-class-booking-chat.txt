P: Boutique fitness studios, whether yoga, Pilates, cycling, boxing or barre, live on first visits. A person who books a trial class, has a good time and joins is worth far more than the price of the class. But between the first thought and the first class sit dozens of small doubts: is it suitable for a beginner, what do I wear, how much are memberships, can I cancel, is there parking. An AI chat on your website and social links can answer those doubts instantly, at any hour, and steer the visitor to book. This guide explains how a studio can use it, what to prepare, how to handle the difference between class booking and appointment booking, and how to measure what matters.
H2[the-questions-that-stop-people-booking]: The questions that stop people booking
TABLE:
H: Group | Typical questions
R: Suitability | Is it OK for beginners? I have an injury. I am pregnant. Is it too hard?
R: Practicalities | What do I wear or bring? Do you have showers, towels, mats? Where do I park?
R: Price | How much is a trial? What are memberships and class packs? Is there a joining fee?
R: Commitment | Can I pause or cancel? What is the late-cancel policy?
R: Schedule | What time are classes? Which suit a first-timer?
R: Instructors and atmosphere | Who teaches? Is it friendly? Is it competitive?
P: Each of these is answerable from a short document. A visitor who gets a warm, clear answer at 10 p.m. is far more likely to book than one who fills a form and waits.
H2[what-to-give-the-assistant]: What to give the assistant
OL:
- **Class descriptions:** what each class is, its intensity, who it suits and what to expect.
- **First-timer guide:** what to wear, arrive early, what happens in the first class.
- **Pricing:** trial offers, single classes, class packs and memberships, with conditions.
- **Policies:** booking, cancellation, late arrival and freezing memberships.
- **Timetable summary:** which classes run when, and which are best for beginners.
- **Facilities and practicalities:** parking, showers, lockers, accessibility.
- **Health and safety statements** you are comfortable making, with clear limits.
P: Write in your studio's voice, warm and encouraging. See [the questions every service business chatbot should answer well](/blog/essential-chatbot-questions-service-business) for a test list.
H2[class-booking-versus-appointment-booking]: Class booking versus appointment booking
P: It helps to be clear about a difference. Classes have capacity: a studio might allow 20 people per class, with a waitlist. Appointments have a single client per slot. Most boutique studios manage classes in a dedicated system, such as their class booking software.
P: Crescelo's built-in booking picker is designed for appointments, with slots from your opening hours and one slot length. It does not manage class capacity or waitlists. For classes, the practical approaches are these.
TABLE:
H: Approach | How it works
R: Assistant answers and links to your class system | The assistant handles questions, then gives the trial booking link for your class software
R: Trial as an appointment | Offer an introductory session or studio tour as a bookable appointment slot with the built-in picker, or with a Calendly or Cal.com link
R: Lead capture | The assistant collects name, contact and preferred class, and the team books them into the class system
P: Choose the one that matches your operation. Many studios use the assistant as the friendly front door, and their class software for the actual booking.
H2[converting-trial-enquiries]: Converting trial enquiries
P: The conversation you want looks like this: a visitor asks a question, gets a confident answer, hears about the trial offer and books or leaves contact details. The assistant should mention the trial once it is relevant, not every message, and should make the next step easy.
TABLE:
H: Visitor says | Assistant does
R: I have never done Pilates. Is that OK? | Reassures with facts from your beginner guide; suggests the best first class; offers the trial
R: How much is it? | Gives the trial price and membership options; asks whether they want to book a trial
R: I have a bad back. | Shares your general guidance, says it cannot give medical advice, suggests speaking to the instructor and offers to pass their details on
R: Can I bring a friend? | Explains your policy and how to book two spots
P: See [conversational lead capture](/blog/conversational-lead-capture) for how to ask for contact details naturally.
H2[memberships-packs-and-policies]: Memberships, packs and policies
P: Membership questions are where clarity matters most, because misunderstandings cause complaints and cancellations. Write your terms plainly: minimum term, how to pause, notice period, what happens on holiday, how late cancellation and no-shows are treated. Then test the assistant with awkward questions. If a term is complex, have it explain the essentials and hand over to a person for the rest. See [effective cancellation policies for service businesses](/blog/effective-cancellation-policies-service-businesses).
H2[health-and-safety-boundaries]: Health and safety boundaries
UL:
- The assistant must not give medical advice or say whether an exercise is safe for a condition.
- It should encourage people with injuries, pregnancy or health conditions to speak to a doctor and to tell the instructor, in words you approve.
- It should hand over any detailed health disclosure to a person, rather than collecting details in chat.
- It should not promise results such as weight loss.
P: Keep sensitive information out of the chat where you can, and follow your local privacy rules. See the [GDPR checklist for website chatbots](/blog/website-chatbot-gdpr-checklist).

H2[a-sample-conversation-with-a-first-timer]: A sample conversation with a first-timer
TABLE:
H: Speaker | Message
R: Visitor | Hi, I have never tried reformer Pilates. Is it very hard, and how much is a first class?
R: Assistant | Hi, welcome. Many of our members started as complete beginners. Our Foundations class is the best first step: it is slower, with plenty of guidance. A trial class is $25. Would you like to see this week's Foundations times?
R: Visitor | Yes, and do I need my own socks?
R: Assistant | Grip socks are required for hygiene, and you can buy a pair at the studio for $8. Foundations runs Tuesday 6:30 p.m., Thursday 9:30 a.m. and Saturday 10:00 a.m. Here is the link to book your trial. Would you like me to take your email so we can send a reminder and what to expect?
P: The assistant reassured a nervous beginner, recommended the right class, stated the price and practical details, and gave the booking link. It also offered a soft follow-up, which captures the lead if the visitor does not book at once.
H2[ideas-to-make-the-trial-offer-work-harder]: Ideas to make the trial offer work harder
UL:
- Mention the trial once the visitor shows interest, not in every message.
- Offer a first-timer guide by email to people who are not ready to book.
- Follow up after a trial with a warm message and a membership suggestion, with your own messaging tools.
- Ask new members how they found you, to learn which channels bring them.
H2[a-launch-checklist-for-studios]: A launch checklist for studios
UL:
- Class descriptions, first-timer guide, prices and policies loaded and tested.
- The trial booking link tested from the chat on a phone.
- Health and safety wording approved by your team, and handover tested.
- Bio link and hosted page set up and tested.
- Someone owns the weekly review of conversations.
- You know how a trial enquiry reaches your team and who follows up within a day.
H2[seasonal-peaks-january-and-september]: Seasonal peaks: January and September
P: Fitness enquiries spike around New Year and the return to school. Prepare in advance: update prices and offers, add short answers about resolutions and beginner options and check that the trial link and lead alerts work. An assistant that answers instantly during the rush is worth most when your team is busiest, and a quick review of the conversations after the peak shows which objections came up so you can address them in your next campaign.
