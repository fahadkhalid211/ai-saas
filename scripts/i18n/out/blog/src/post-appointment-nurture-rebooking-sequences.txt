P: Most service businesses put their effort into getting the first booking and very little into the second. Yet a returning customer costs far less to win than a new one, and many services are naturally repeatable: haircuts every six weeks, dental check-ups twice a year, boiler services annually, massages monthly. A short, well-timed follow-up after the appointment turns one visit into a habit. This guide covers what to send and when, how to ask for a review without being pushy, how to make rebooking a single step, what the law expects of marketing messages and how a chat assistant with a booking link supports the whole loop. It is honest about what a chat assistant does and does not do: it answers and books; the follow-up messages themselves are usually sent from your own email or messaging tools.
H2[the-three-follow-ups-worth-doing]: The three follow-ups worth doing
TABLE:
H: Follow-up | Timing | Purpose
R: Thank you and aftercare | Same day or next morning | Reassure, give aftercare, invite questions
R: Review request | A few days later, once the result is visible | Collect honest reviews
R: Rebooking prompt | Shortly before the customer is typically due | Bring them back at the right moment
P: Three light touches beat a stream of promotional messages.
H2[1-thank-you-and-aftercare]: 1. Thank you and aftercare
UL:
- Thank them by name and mention the service.
- Give the two or three aftercare points customers most often forget.
- Invite questions and say how to ask: reply to the message or use the chat on your website.
- Keep it short enough to read on a phone.
P: This message also reduces unnecessary calls, especially when your assistant has the aftercare instructions in its knowledge base, so a question about what to do tonight gets an accurate answer at any hour.
H2[2-the-review-request]: 2. The review request
UL:
- Wait until the customer has had time to see the result.
- Ask once, with a direct link to the review page.
- Ask everyone, not only those who seem happy; selective asking can breach review-platform rules and consumer law in some places.
- Never offer payment or discounts in return for positive reviews.
- Thank people who review, and respond professionally to criticism.
P: Reviews feed your reputation and give your assistant real proof to point to; see [using social proof in chat](/blog/social-proof-reviews-ai-chat).
H2[3-the-rebooking-prompt]: 3. The rebooking prompt
P: Work out each service's natural cycle and message a little before it ends. A haircut customer due in six weeks might hear from you at week five: 'It is nearly time for your next cut. Here are the times we have free; book here in a minute.' The essential part is making rebooking one step: a direct link to your booking page, or an invitation to open the chat and ask for a slot.
TABLE:
H: Service | Example cycle | Prompt at
R: Haircut | 5 to 6 weeks | Week 5
R: Dental check-up | 6 months | Month 5
R: Annual boiler service | 12 months | Month 11
R: Massage | 4 weeks | Week 3 to 4
R: Pet grooming | 6 to 8 weeks | Week 5 to 6
P: These cycles are examples; use the ones your own customers follow.
H2[making-rebooking-easy]: Making rebooking easy
OL:
- Put a booking link in every message.
- Let the customer pick a time without phoning.
- Offer the same service again by default, with the option to change.
- Send a confirmation with a private link to change or cancel; Crescelo's booking confirmation emails include this, along with reminders before the appointment.
- Make sure the assistant can answer 'Can I book the same again?' correctly.
H2[where-the-chat-assistant-fits]: Where the chat assistant fits
UL:
- It answers aftercare questions from your documents at any hour.
- It books a new appointment when the customer visits your site or a hosted page.
- It captures details for a callback when a person is needed.
- It cannot send scheduled follow-up messages itself; use your email tool, calendar reminders or a simple list for that.
P: Be clear about this division of labour so you do not expect features that are not there. Booking confirmations and reminders by email are sent by the platform's booking flow; marketing follow-ups are yours to send.
H2[consent-and-the-rules]: Consent and the rules
UL:
- Service messages tied to an appointment, such as confirmations and aftercare, are treated differently from marketing in many places. Marketing generally needs consent.
- Get consent when you collect details, and record it.
- Give an easy way to stop receiving messages, and honour it.
- Keep messages accurate and identify your business.
- Rules differ by country and by channel (email, text, phone). Check yours, and read [privacy law for lead capture](/blog/chatbot-lead-capture-gdpr-ccpa-compliance).
P: This is general information, not legal advice.
H2[a-simple-system-you-can-run-today]: A simple system you can run today
OL:
- Choose your top three repeat services and their cycles.
- Write three short templates: aftercare, review request, rebooking prompt.
- After each appointment, note the date; set a reminder to send each message.
- Use a spreadsheet or your booking calendar to track who is due.
- Every month, count how many customers rebooked after a prompt and adjust timing.
P: Start with the rebooking prompt for your most frequent service, since it produces revenue most directly.
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Messaging too often.
- Asking for reviews immediately, before the result shows.
- Sending marketing without consent.
- Making rebooking a phone call.
- Ignoring feedback in replies.
- Expecting the chat assistant to send scheduled messages by itself.
H2[a-sample-set-of-messages]: A sample set of messages
TABLE:
H: Message | Example
R: Thank you and aftercare | Thanks for coming in, Sam. For the next 48 hours, avoid hot water on the treated area. Any questions, reply here or ask on our website chat.
R: Review request | We would love to hear how it went. If you have a minute, you can leave a review here: [link].
R: Rebooking prompt | It is nearly time for your next visit. You can pick a time here: [link].
P: Adjust the wording to your voice, keep each message under a few short sentences and make sure the links work on a phone before you send them.
H2[measuring-whether-it-works]: Measuring whether it works
UL:
- Count how many customers rebook within two weeks of a prompt.
- Compare repeat visit rates before and after you began following up.
- Note the review count and average rating over time.
- Ask new customers how they found you, to see the effect of reviews.
- Adjust the timing if many customers book just before or long after the prompt.
P: Small changes, such as sending the prompt a week earlier, can noticeably alter the results, and you will only see it if you keep simple records.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: When should I follow up after an appointment?
A: Send thanks and aftercare the same or next day, a review request a few days later and a rebooking prompt shortly before the customer is due again.
Q: How do I ask for reviews without being pushy?
A: Ask once, with a direct link, ask everyone and never offer incentives for positive reviews.
Q: Can Crescelo send follow-up campaigns?
A: It sends booking confirmations and reminders. Marketing follow-ups are sent from your own email or messaging tools; the assistant answers questions and books appointments.
Q: Do I need consent to send follow-ups?
A: Appointment-related service messages are treated differently from marketing, but marketing generally needs consent. Rules vary by country, so check yours.
Q: How do I make rebooking easy?
A: Include a booking link in every follow-up and let customers choose a time in one step.
Q: How do I know it is working?
A: Count how many customers rebooked after a prompt, and compare with your baseline.
