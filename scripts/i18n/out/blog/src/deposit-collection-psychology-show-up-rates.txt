P: Deposits are the most discussed tool for reducing no-shows, and also the most contentious. Supporters say that asking clients to pay something in advance makes them value the appointment and show up. Critics say deposits put off new customers, create refund disputes and add admin work. Both are partly right. The honest answer is that deposits can help in some businesses and hurt in others, and that cheaper measures such as clear reminders and easy rescheduling often deliver much of the benefit with far less friction. This guide explains why deposits might change behaviour, what the trade-offs are, which types of business use them successfully, what alternatives to try first and how to test a change without guessing. It also states plainly that Crescelo's built-in booking does not collect deposits or payments today, so deposits, if you use them, are handled by your own payment method.
H2[why-a-deposit-might-change-behaviour]: Why a deposit might change behaviour
UL:
- **Commitment:** paying something makes the booking feel real, not a casual reservation.
- **Loss aversion:** people dislike losing money they have already paid more than they like the idea of gaining it back.
- **Signalling:** a deposit tells the client that your time is valuable and the slot cannot easily be replaced.
- **Filtering:** it discourages people who were never going to come, at the cost of some who might have.
P: Behavioural research on commitment supports these effects in general, but results in a specific business depend on price, customer type and competition. Be careful with claims of precise percentages from vendors; they rarely match your situation.
H2[the-trade-offs]: The trade-offs
TABLE:
H: Benefit | Cost
R: Fewer casual bookings that turn into no-shows | Some genuine customers abandon the booking at the payment step
R: Income protected when someone fails to attend | Refunds and disputes to manage
R: Signals professionalism for high-value services | May feel unwelcoming for low-cost or first-time services
R: Helps with expensive materials or specialist time | Payment processing fees and admin
R: Discourages serial no-shows | Requires a clear, fair policy and consistent enforcement
P: A deposit is an extra step in the booking flow. Every extra step loses some visitors, which is why you should only add one where the cost of no-shows justifies it.
H2[where-deposits-tend-to-make-sense]: Where deposits tend to make sense
UL:
- Long or expensive appointments, such as several hours of colour work or a tattoo session.
- Services with costly preparation or materials.
- High-demand slots where a no-show blocks someone else.
- Group classes and events with limited places.
- Businesses that have had repeated no-shows despite reminders.
H2[where-they-may-not]: Where they may not
UL:
- Short, low-priced services where the deposit is a large share of the price.
- New businesses building trust, where friction costs more than an occasional gap.
- Services people book on impulse, where a payment step loses conversions.
- Situations where you cannot handle refunds quickly and fairly.
H2[cheaper-things-to-try-first]: Cheaper things to try first
TABLE:
H: Measure | Why it helps | Effort
R: Email reminders before the appointment | Removes forgetfulness, the most common cause | Low; Crescelo sends email reminders
R: A private link to change or cancel | Removes the awkwardness of calling | Low; included in Crescelo's confirmation email
R: A clear cancellation policy in the confirmation | Sets expectations kindly | Low
R: Confirming by reply for long appointments | Adds a small commitment | Medium
R: A short waiting list to refill slots | Reduces the cost of a cancellation | Low
R: Personal follow-up for repeat no-shows | Addresses the cause | Medium

H2[if-you-do-take-deposits]: If you do take deposits
OL:
- Keep the amount modest but meaningful, for example a fixed sum or a small percentage.
- State clearly what it covers, whether it is deducted from the final price and when it is refundable.
- Explain the policy before the client commits, not afterwards.
- Use a reputable payment provider and comply with its terms and local consumer law.
- Refund promptly when the client gives proper notice.
- Be consistent, but allow reasonable exceptions for real emergencies.
- Record deposits in your own system, since Crescelo's booking does not handle payments.
H2[test-rather-than-guess]: Test rather than guess
TABLE:
H: Step | How
R: Baseline | Measure your no-show rate for two months
R: Change one thing | Improve reminders and the change link first
R: Measure again | Compare no-shows and rebookings
R: Then consider a deposit | Only for the services where no-shows remain costly
R: Pilot it | Try it for one service or one day of the week and compare booking volume as well as no-shows
P: Watch both sides of the ledger: no-shows avoided and bookings lost. A deposit that prevents ten no-shows but deters twenty bookings is not a win.
H2[wording-that-keeps-goodwill]: Wording that keeps goodwill
P: Example: 'To hold your appointment we take a $20 deposit, which is deducted from your final bill. If you need to change or cancel, let us know at least 24 hours before and we will refund it in full or move it to a new date.' It is short, fair and tells the client what happens in each case.
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Adding a deposit before trying reminders.
- Keeping the policy in fine print.
- Refusing refunds for legitimate emergencies.
- Applying rules unevenly.
- Not measuring conversions after the change.
- Claiming your system collects deposits when it does not.
H2[a-worked-comparison]: A worked comparison
P: Imagine a studio with 100 bookings a month and eight no-shows, each costing $60 in lost income, a monthly loss of $480. Better reminders and a change link cut no-shows to five, saving $180. A deposit might cut them to two, saving a further $180, but if it also deters five of the hundred bookings at $60 each, that is a $300 loss, more than the gain. The numbers here are illustrative, not predictions; the point is to run the same sum with your own figures before deciding.
TABLE:
H: Scenario | No-shows | Bookings | Approximate monthly result
R: Today | 8 | 100 | Baseline
R: Reminders and change link | 5 | 100 | Better by about $180
R: Plus deposit | 2 | 95 | Depends on your own numbers
P: Where the sum is close, choose the option that keeps the booking process simplest for customers.
H2[alternatives-that-keep-some-commitment]: Alternatives that keep some commitment
UL:
- Ask clients to confirm by replying to the reminder for long or expensive appointments.
- Take card details on file, if your payment provider allows it, and state that a fee applies only to late cancellations.
- Offer a small discount or a priority slot to clients who confirm early.
- Require a deposit only from clients who have missed an appointment before, and explain why kindly.
P: Targeted measures like these protect your time without adding a payment step for every new customer, and they are easier to explain and to reverse if they do not work.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: Do deposits reduce no-shows?
A: They can, especially for long or expensive appointments, but they also add friction and may deter bookings. Try reminders and easy rescheduling first, and measure the effect.
Q: Does Crescelo collect deposits?
A: No. The built-in booking does not take payments or deposits. Use your own payment process if you need one.
Q: What is the cheapest way to reduce no-shows?
A: Email reminders and a simple link to change or cancel.
Q: How much should a deposit be?
A: Enough to matter to the client without being a barrier; many businesses use a fixed amount or a modest percentage.
Q: Are deposits legal?
A: Generally yes, but consumer protection rules vary. State terms clearly and check the rules where you operate.
Q: How do I know whether it worked?
A: Compare no-show rate and total bookings before and after, over a few months.
