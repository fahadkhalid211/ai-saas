P: Local search is where many service businesses win or lose customers. Someone types 'dentist near me' or 'plumber open now', looks at a map with three results and picks one. Getting into those results is local SEO. But visibility is only half the job: a visitor who arrives and cannot find your price, hours or a way to book will go to the next name. This blueprint joins the two halves. It covers how to be found locally, what your profile and website must show, how to earn and use reviews, and how to convert visitors with clear pages, fast answers and easy booking, including chat that works at any hour.
H2[how-local-search-works-briefly]: How local search works, briefly
P: Google's local results depend broadly on three things: relevance (does your business match the search), distance (how close it is to the searcher) and prominence (how well known and trusted it is, including reviews and links). You cannot change distance, but you can improve relevance and prominence, and you can make sure that visitors who find you convert.
TABLE:
H: Factor | What you control
R: Relevance | Accurate categories, services, descriptions and content
R: Distance | A correct address and service area
R: Prominence | Reviews, quality links, mentions and a strong website
H2[step-1-get-your-google-business-profile-right]: Step 1: Get your Google Business Profile right
UL:
- Claim and verify the profile.
- Choose the most accurate primary category and add secondary ones.
- List every service, with descriptions and prices or ranges where you can.
- Keep name, address and phone consistent with your website and other listings.
- Set accurate hours, including holidays.
- Add real photos of your premises, team and work.
- Link to a page that helps the visitor act: booking, services or your assistant.
- Use posts and updates for offers and news, sparingly.
P: Do not stuff keywords into the business name; it breaks guidelines and can lead to suspension.
H2[step-2-make-your-website-match]: Step 2: Make your website match
TABLE:
H: Page | Local SEO job | Conversion job
R: Home | Say what you do and where | One clear action
R: Service pages | One page per main service with local wording | Price, proof, booking
R: Location or service area pages | Real information about the areas you serve | Show you know the area; link to booking
R: Contact | Consistent address, phone and hours | Click-to-call, map, form and chat
R: About | Trust and credentials | Faces and facts
P: Write for people first. Mention the area naturally, and avoid near-duplicate pages for every suburb, which are weak and can be penalised.
H2[step-3-earn-and-use-reviews]: Step 3: Earn and use reviews
UL:
- Ask every happy customer for a review, at the moment they are most satisfied.
- Make it easy: send a direct link.
- Reply to every review, kindly and briefly, including the critical ones.
- Never buy or fake reviews; it is against the rules and can destroy trust.
- Show a selection of real reviews on your site with permission.
P: Reviews drive both ranking and conversion. See [social proof and reviews in AI chat](/blog/social-proof-reviews-ai-chat).
H2[step-4-prepare-for-the-mobile-visitor]: Step 4: Prepare for the mobile visitor
P: Most local searches are on phones. A visitor from a map is often close to buying: they want your number, hours, directions and a quick way to book. Make sure the page loads fast, the phone number is a tappable link, the address opens in maps, the hours are visible and the booking action is thumb-sized. Test it on a real phone on mobile data.
H2[step-5-answer-questions-instantly]: Step 5: Answer questions instantly
P: Local visitors ask the same questions: are you open now, how much, do you cover my area, can I book today. A chat on your site, and on the hosted page you link from your profile, can answer those from your own documents at any hour and offer a booking. See [converting Google Business Profile traffic in chat](/blog/convert-google-business-traffic-chat) and [service area verification in chat](/blog/service-area-verification-ai-chat).
H2[step-6-make-booking-effortless]: Step 6: Make booking effortless
UL:
- Offer online booking with real availability.
- Confirm immediately and send a change link.
- Offer a phone option for those who prefer it.
- Make sure the profile's booking link goes straight to the booking step.
- Keep the fields short.
P: See [AI appointment booking](/blog/ai-appointment-booking) and the [appointment booking feature](/product/appointment-booking).
H2[step-7-measure-what-matters]: Step 7: Measure what matters
TABLE:
H: Metric | Where to find it
R: Profile views, calls, direction requests, website clicks | Google Business Profile insights
R: Rankings for key local searches | Manual checks from a phone in your area, or a local rank tracker
R: Website visitors from search and maps | Analytics
R: Bookings and enquiries by source | Booking and chat reports, plus a 'how did you find us' question
R: Reviews count and rating | Your profile
P: Review monthly and change one thing at a time.
H2[common-local-seo-mistakes]: Common local SEO mistakes
UL:
- Inconsistent name, address or phone across listings.
- Unverified or duplicate profiles.
- Keyword-stuffed business names.
- Thin location pages copied for every suburb.
- Ignoring reviews.
- A slow, hard-to-use mobile site.
- No clear next step after the click.

TABLE:
H: Week | Focus
R: 1 | Audit your profile and listings; fix name, address, phone, hours and categories
R: 2 | Improve your service and contact pages: prices, proof, tappable phone and booking
R: 3 | Start a review routine; reply to existing reviews; add photos
R: 4 | Add chat and booking where visitors hesitate; review the numbers
H2[worked-example-a-physiotherapy-clinic]: Worked example: a physiotherapy clinic
P: A clinic in a mid-sized town ranks fourth in the map results for 'physiotherapy near me'. Its profile has the wrong category, photos from years ago, and a website link to a slow home page. Over a month it makes changes in order of effort. It corrects the primary category and adds services with descriptions. It adds current photos and a short weekly post. It starts asking every discharged patient for a review with a direct link, and replies to each one. It points the profile link to a fast services page with prices, tappable phone and an assistant that answers insurance and referral questions and offers appointments. Calls, direction requests and bookings all rise, and the clinic climbs to second place. No single change did it; the improvements reinforced each other.
TABLE:
H: Change | Effort | Effect
R: Fix categories and services | 1 hour | Better relevance for the searches that matter
R: Fresh photos | 2 hours | More clicks and trust
R: Review routine | 10 minutes a week | Stronger prominence and conversion
R: Faster services page with chat | Half a day | More visitors become bookings
H2[what-to-do-about-competitors]: What to do about competitors
P: Look at the three businesses above you in the map results. Study their categories, services, photos, review counts and how they respond to reviews. Note what they do better and what they do not do at all. You do not need to copy them; find the gaps. If none of them shows prices, showing yours is an advantage. If none answers questions out of hours, a chat that does is one. Revisit the comparison every quarter.
H2[local-content-that-earns-attention]: Local content that earns attention
UL:
- Answer the questions customers ask, on your own site: how much, how long, what to expect.
- Write about local situations: seasonal problems, local regulations, local events you serve.
- Share real before-and-after work with permission.
- Sponsor or support local groups and ask for a link where sensible.
- Keep everything truthful and specific; generic filler helps no one.
H2[keeping-it-going]: Keeping it going
P: Local SEO is a habit, not a project. Put four recurring tasks in your calendar: a weekly review request routine, a monthly check that hours and details are still correct everywhere, a quarterly look at competitors and a yearly refresh of photos and service descriptions. Assign each to a named person. The businesses that keep their profile and site current tend to hold their rankings, while those that set it up once and forget it slowly slip as competitors keep improving.
H2[questions-to-ask-yourself-each-quarter]: Questions to ask yourself each quarter
UL:
- If a stranger searched for what we do in our area today, would they find us, and would they choose us?
- What do our three closest competitors show that we do not?
- What is the most common question customers ask that our website and profile do not answer?
- How fast can a mobile visitor from the map book or call?
- What would make a happy customer more likely to leave a review this month?
P: Write the answers down and pick one improvement for the next month. Small, steady progress beats an annual overhaul.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: What is local SEO?
A: Improving your visibility in local search results and maps, mainly through your Google Business Profile, your website and reviews.
Q: How do I rank higher in the map results?
A: Complete and verify your profile, keep your details consistent, earn reviews, and have a helpful, fast website that matches what searchers want.
Q: Do I need a separate page for each suburb?
A: Only if you have real, useful information for each. Thin duplicate pages do not help.
Q: How important are reviews?
A: Very. They influence ranking and are often the deciding factor for a customer.
Q: How can chat help local conversion?
A: It answers questions like hours, price and coverage instantly, at any hour, and can book the visitor before they go elsewhere.
Q: How do I measure local SEO?
A: Use profile insights, analytics, booking and chat reports, and ask new customers how they found you.
