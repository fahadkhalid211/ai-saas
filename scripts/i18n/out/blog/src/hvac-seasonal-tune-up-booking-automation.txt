P: HVAC work comes in waves. In a heatwave or the first cold snap, every phone rings at once and emergency calls swamp the schedule. In quieter weeks, crews wait for work. Seasonal tune-ups, a spring air-conditioning check and an autumn heating check, are the tool that smooths those waves: they fill slow periods, reduce breakdowns in busy ones and turn one-off customers into regulars. The challenge is that tune-ups are easy to forget for both sides. Customers do not think about the furnace until it fails, and busy offices forget to contact past customers. This guide explains how to use reminders, a booking chat and simple follow-up habits to fill seasonal schedules, how to describe plans and prices clearly, how to manage capacity and how to measure the result.
H2[why-tune-ups-are-worth-the-effort]: Why tune-ups are worth the effort
UL:
- **Smoother workload:** tune-ups can be booked into quieter weeks, balancing the year.
- **Fewer emergencies:** systems that are checked fail less often at the worst moment.
- **Customer loyalty:** a yearly visit keeps your name in front of the customer.
- **Upsell opportunities:** honest recommendations during a visit lead to repair and replacement work.
- **Predictable revenue:** maintenance plans give steady income.
P: The point is not to sell things people do not need. It is to make a helpful, well-timed service easy to say yes to.
H2[who-to-contact-and-when]: Who to contact and when
TABLE:
H: Segment | Message | Timing
R: Past customers | It is time for your annual check | Six to eight weeks before the season starts
R: Plan members | Your included visit is due, here are times | At the start of the season
R: Recent installs | First check after installation, if included | As your warranty terms suggest
R: Website visitors | Answers about tune-ups, with a booking option | All year
P: Send reminders before demand peaks, not during. Many companies start spring air-conditioning reminders in early spring and heating reminders in early autumn, adjusted to their climate. Use your own customer records and respect consent rules for marketing messages; see [GDPR and CCPA for chatbot lead capture](/blog/chatbot-lead-capture-gdpr-ccpa-compliance).
H2[what-the-booking-chat-should-answer]: What the booking chat should answer
TABLE:
H: Question | A good answer
R: What does a tune-up include? | A clear checklist of what your technician does
R: How much is it? | Price or range, and whether plan members pay less
R: How long does it take? | Typical duration
R: Do I need one every year? | Your recommendation, in general terms and without scare tactics
R: What is a maintenance plan? | What is included, the price and how to join
R: Can you come this week? | Real available times
R: Do you service my brand? | Brands you service, or that the team will check
P: Load a short document for each into the assistant's knowledge. Write in plain words and avoid claims you cannot support, such as a specific efficiency saving. See [preparing your service menu and pricing documents](/blog/prepare-service-menu-pricing-docs-rag).
H2[describe-maintenance-plans-clearly]: Describe maintenance plans clearly
UL:
- What visits are included and when.
- What is discounted, such as repairs or parts, and by how much.
- Whether priority service is included and how it works in a busy period.
- The price per year or month and the minimum term.
- How to cancel.
- What is not covered.
P: Vague plans create disputes. A one-page plain description helps sales and prevents misunderstandings. The assistant should state it exactly and not improvise.
H2[control-capacity]: Control capacity
P: The risk of a successful campaign is overload: a hundred tune-up bookings the week the first heatwave hits. Decide how many tune-ups you will book per day and per crew, and set your booking hours and slot length to match. Leave room for emergency calls, and stop offering tune-up slots in weeks when breakdown demand is likely. Crescelo's built-in picker uses your opening hours and one slot length, and hides taken slots; for capacity per crew or per day, use your own scheduling process or Calendly or Cal.com links. H2[capture-the-emergency-call-too]: Capture the emergency call too
P: The same chat catches breakdown enquiries. For a system that has stopped, the assistant should recognise urgency, give safe guidance, capture the details and alert your on-call technician. For a gas smell or carbon monoxide alarm, it should tell the customer to leave the building and call the emergency services, not to wait for a technician. H2[reminders-after-booking]: Reminders after booking
OL:
- Immediate confirmation with the date, window, what the technician will do and what the customer should prepare, such as access to the unit.
- A reminder the day before with a change link.
- After the visit, a summary of what was done and any recommendations.
- A note for next year: 'We will remind you in March.'

H2[what-technicians-should-do-on-the-visit]: What technicians should do on the visit
P: The tune-up is also the moment customers judge you. Technicians should arrive in the window, explain what they will check, leave the area tidy and give a plain summary. Recommendations should be honest and prioritised: what is urgent, what can wait and what is simply worth knowing. Customers who feel informed rather than pressured are far more likely to renew and to refer neighbours.

H2[a-worked-example-filling-the-autumn-schedule]: A worked example: filling the autumn schedule
P: A four-technician HVAC company has 900 past customers and 120 plan members. In early September it sends a short email to each: 'Your annual heating check is due. Book a time that suits you.' The link opens a page with the assistant, which answers questions about what the visit includes and what it costs, and offers times. Booking hours are set to allow two tune-ups per technician per day, leaving the rest of the day for breakdowns. In the first two weeks, 140 tune-ups are booked, about a third of them outside office hours. A second reminder to those who did not respond adds another 60. The busy season arrives with the schedule already full of planned work, and the emergency line is less overloaded than in previous years because more systems were checked.

H2[scripts-for-the-follow-up-conversation]: Scripts for the follow-up conversation
UL:
- **Reminder email:** 'Your annual heating check is due. It takes about an hour and helps avoid breakdowns in the cold. Choose a time here.'
- **In-chat:** 'A tune-up covers filters, safety checks, efficiency and a written report. It is $X for one system, and plan members pay $Y. Would you like to see times?'
- **After the visit:** 'Thank you. Here is a summary of what we checked. We noted [item]; I can send a quote if you wish. We will remind you next autumn.'
P: Keep messages short and helpful, and never invent urgency.
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Promoting tune-ups during a heatwave, when crews are needed for breakdowns.
- Vague plan descriptions that lead to disputes about what is included.
- Booking more tune-ups than the crews can complete.
- Forgetting to update prices and lead times in the assistant's documents.
- Sending reminders to customers who asked not to be contacted.
- Never checking whether reminders lead to bookings.
P: A short pre-season checklist prevents most of these: documents current, capacity set, reminders scheduled, consent checked and alerts tested.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: How do I fill my HVAC tune-up schedule?
A: Contact past customers and plan members before the season, make booking easy in a chat or booking page and manage capacity so emergencies are protected.
Q: Can a chatbot sell maintenance plans?
A: It can explain the plan from your document and take an enquiry. Keep the description exact, and let a person handle sign-up and payment.
Q: Should the chat handle heating emergencies?
A: It should recognise urgency, give safe guidance, capture details and alert your on-call technician. For gas or carbon monoxide risks it should direct customers to emergency services.
Q: How many reminders should I send?
A: Usually two before the season, with a confirmation and a day-before reminder for booked visits.
Q: How do I avoid overbooking?
A: Set booking hours and slot length to match crew capacity, and pause promotion when breakdown demand is high.
Q: Does Crescelo manage crews?
A: No. Its built-in picker uses your opening hours and one slot length. For crew-level scheduling, use your own system or Calendly or Cal.com links.
