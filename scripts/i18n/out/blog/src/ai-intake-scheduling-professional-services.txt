P: Professional services firms live on consultations. A prospective client who cannot get a straight answer or a booking quickly will contact another firm, and the enquiry that arrives on a Sunday evening is often the most anxious and the most valuable. An AI assistant can greet visitors, explain what you do and how you charge, capture the basics of the matter and book an initial consultation, while making clear that it is not giving advice. This guide covers how law firms, accountants, financial advisers and consultants can use one responsibly.
NOTE: This article is general information, not legal advice. Professional conduct rules on advertising, confidentiality and client communication vary by jurisdiction and profession. Check the rules that apply to you and, where needed, ask your regulator or a professional adviser before deploying any tool that talks to the public.
H2[what-the-assistant-should-and-should-not-do]: What the assistant should and should not do
TABLE:
H: It should | It should not
R: Explain your practice areas and how the process works | Give legal, tax or financial advice
R: State how you charge, in the terms you publish | Quote a fee for a specific matter unless you have approved it
R: Capture contact details and a brief description of the enquiry | Ask for confidential details or documents in the chat
R: Book an initial consultation | Promise outcomes or timelines
R: Say clearly that it is an AI and that no advice is given or lawyer-client relationship formed by the chat | Pretend to be a lawyer or adviser
R: Hand over urgent matters to a person | Assess the merits of a case
P: This is a front door, not a fee earner. Its value is speed, consistency and capture.
H2[confidentiality-and-the-first-message]: Confidentiality and the first message
P: Visitors, especially those with sensitive problems, often type more than they should. Design the opening to prevent that. A short notice such as 'I am an AI assistant. I can explain our services and book a consultation. Please do not share confidential details here; we will discuss your matter in the consultation' sets the right expectation. The assistant should then steer away from details and toward booking. It should also state that the chat does not create a client relationship. Have your professional-conduct or compliance lead approve the wording. See [confidentiality and privilege with an AI website assistant](/blog/confidentiality-attorney-client-privilege-ai-chat).
H2[intake-that-saves-the-first-ten-minutes]: Intake that saves the first ten minutes
P: Well-designed intake collects just enough to prepare and to check for conflicts, without turning into a legal questionnaire. A sensible minimum is below.
TABLE:
H: Field | Why
R: Name and preferred contact method | To reply and book
R: Practice area or type of matter, in the visitor's words | To route to the right person
R: Urgency or key dates (a hearing, a deadline) | To prioritise
R: Other parties' names, if you check conflicts at intake | Conflict check; ask only if you need it and only what you need
R: How they found you | Marketing insight
P: Send this to your intake team by email or into your case management system with the conversation attached. See [legal intake automation and conflict checks](/blog/legal-intake-automation-conflict-checks).
H2[speed-matters-especially-for-urgent-matters]: Speed matters, especially for urgent matters
P: In areas such as personal injury, family or criminal law, immigration and employment, people search when something has just happened, and they contact several firms. Response time is often the deciding factor. An assistant that replies in seconds, explains your process and books a call within the hour can be the difference. H2[booking-initial-consultations]: Booking initial consultations
OL:
- Define consultation types: free 15-minute call, paid 45-minute meeting, in-person or video.
- Set who conducts each and when.
- Set buffers and preparation time.
- Consider a small fee or deposit for longer consultations, where lawful and appropriate in your profession.
- Send a confirmation with what to prepare, who they will meet and how to change the time.
P: Booking inside the chat catches the visitor when they are ready. See [AI appointment booking](/blog/ai-appointment-booking).
H2[data-protection]: Data protection
UL:
- Tell visitors they are talking to an AI and link to your privacy policy.
- Collect the minimum and avoid sensitive details in chat.
- Ask any vendor where data is stored, who processes it and how to delete it.
- Choose your own AI provider where possible so you know who sees the messages; with a bring-your-own-key product you decide.
- Set a retention rule and delete what you no longer need.
- Where you serve people in Europe, read the [GDPR checklist](/blog/website-chatbot-gdpr-checklist).
H2[measuring-the-result]: Measuring the result
UL:
- Enquiries received outside office hours, and how many booked.
- Time from enquiry to first response.
- Consultations booked from the website, and conversion to clients.
- Enquiries routed to the wrong practice area, which show where the descriptions are unclear.
- Questions the assistant could not answer, which tell you what to publish.

H2[a-sample-first-conversation]: A sample first conversation
TABLE:
H: Speaker | Message
R: Visitor | I was in a car accident two weeks ago. Can I talk to someone about it?
R: Assistant | I am sorry to hear that. I am an AI assistant for the firm, and I cannot give legal advice, but I can tell you how we work and arrange a free consultation with one of our lawyers. Please do not share details of the accident here; the lawyer will go through them with you. Would you like to book a call?
R: Visitor | Yes, is tomorrow possible?
R: Assistant | I can offer tomorrow at 10:00 or 2:30. What name and number should we use? If anything is urgent, such as an injury that needs care, please contact emergency services or your doctor first.
P: The assistant was empathetic, stated its limits, avoided details, offered real times and included a safety line. The conversation was then handed to the intake team.
H2[wording-that-protects-you]: Wording that protects you
UL:
- A visible statement that the assistant is an AI and does not give legal, tax or financial advice.
- A statement that using the chat does not create a client relationship.
- A request not to share confidential information in the chat.
- A link to your privacy policy.
- A clear route to a person.
P: Have the wording reviewed by whoever handles your firm's compliance, and re-review it when your services change.
H2[what-to-measure]: What to measure
UL:
- Number of enquiries by practice area and how many were routed correctly.
- Time from enquiry to first personal contact.
- Consultations booked and consultations attended.
- Consultations that became clients.
- Common questions that your website does not answer.
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Letting the assistant comment on the merits of a case.
- Asking for detailed facts in the chat.
- Quoting a fee for a specific matter without approval.
- Not testing sensitive scenarios such as domestic abuse or criminal charges.
- Failing to update your descriptions when practice areas change.
P: For sensitive situations, decide in advance what the assistant says and where it directs the visitor, for example to emergency services or a support line, and test those scenarios with your team.
H2[keeping-records]: Keeping records
P: Because professional firms have duties around records, decide up front how long chat transcripts are kept, who can read them and how they are stored. Treat them as part of your intake records where they contain a prospective client's information, and delete them according to your retention policy. Document your decisions.
H2[setting-it-up-in-a-week]: Setting it up in a week
OL:
- Day 1: agree scope and wording with the partner or compliance lead: what the assistant may say, what it must never say and where it hands over.
- Day 2: write the documents: practice areas, how you work, how you charge, what to prepare for a consultation, opening hours.
- Day 3: load them, set the greeting and the no-advice notice, and configure intake fields and alerts.
- Day 4: set consultation types, calendar and confirmations.
- Day 5: test with sensitive scenarios, urgent matters and unanswerable questions, and have a partner try to break it.
- Then launch quietly, review every conversation for two weeks and adjust.
P: A phased launch, first on one practice area or one page, lets you build confidence before you widen it. Keep a log of changes so you can show your regulator or insurer how the tool is governed if asked.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: Can a law firm use an AI chatbot?
A: Yes, for intake and booking, provided the wording is approved, it does not give advice and it complies with your professional conduct and privacy rules.
Q: Does chatting with a bot create a lawyer-client relationship?
A: Your notice should say it does not, and the assistant should not give advice. Have your compliance lead approve the wording, because the rules differ by jurisdiction.
Q: Should the chatbot ask about the details of the case?
A: Keep it to a general description and urgency. Detailed and confidential information belongs in the consultation.
Q: Can it check for conflicts of interest?
A: It can collect the names you need for a conflict check and pass them to your intake team. The check itself should be done by your usual process.
Q: Is it suitable for accountants and financial advisers?
A: Yes, for explaining your process, listing documents and booking consultations, but it should not give tax or financial advice.
Q: How is client data protected?
A: Ask your vendor about storage, sub-processors and deletion, choose your own AI provider where possible and collect only what you need.
