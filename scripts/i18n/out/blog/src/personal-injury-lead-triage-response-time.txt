P: Personal injury is one of the most competitive areas of legal marketing. Someone injured in an accident searches for help, often from a hospital bed or their phone in a car park, and contacts several firms. The first firm to respond with empathy and a clear next step often signs the client. Firms that reply the next morning find the case has gone. Response time is therefore a business-critical measure, and it is one where an automated first response can help. But this is also a sensitive area: visitors may be hurt and distressed, rules on solicitation and advertising are strict, and an automated assistant must never give legal advice or make promises about outcomes. This guide explains how a personal injury firm can use a chat to respond instantly and triage properly: what to say first, what to ask, how to spot urgency, how to route leads and how to stay within the rules.
NOTE: Rules on advertising, solicitation and client communication for personal injury lawyers are strict and vary by jurisdiction. This is general information, not legal advice. Have your ethics partner approve the wording and the process.
H2[why-speed-matters-here]: Why speed matters here
UL:
- Injured people contact several firms and often choose the first that answers.
- Evidence and deadlines are time-sensitive: limitation periods, witness memory, insurance notification.
- A slow response signals that a firm will be slow later.
- Enquiries arrive at night and at weekends, when accidents happen.
P: A prompt, compassionate reply is not a marketing trick. It is service, and it often decides who helps the person. See [speed to lead for service businesses](/blog/speed-to-lead-service-businesses).
H2[what-the-first-message-should-do]: What the first message should do
OL:
- Express concern, briefly and sincerely.
- State that it is an AI assistant that cannot give legal advice.
- Check for immediate medical or safety needs and direct to emergency services if needed.
- Explain what happens next: a case manager will contact them, and when.
- Ask for contact details and a good time to call.
P: Example: 'I am sorry to hear you were hurt. I am an AI assistant and cannot give legal advice. If you need medical help right now, please call emergency services. Otherwise, I can arrange for a member of our team to call you today. What is your name and the best number?'
H2[safety-first]: Safety first
P: Many visitors will write about injuries that need medical attention. The assistant should never assess medical severity. If a visitor describes severe symptoms, or has not yet been seen by a doctor, the first response should encourage them to seek medical care and give emergency numbers. Legal matters can wait for an hour; health cannot.
H2[triage-questions-that-help-the-firm]: Triage questions that help the firm
TABLE:
H: Question | Purpose
R: What happened, in a sentence (type of incident)? | Practice fit and routing
R: When did it happen? | Limitation and urgency
R: Have you received medical treatment? | Care and evidence
R: Was anyone else involved or at fault, in general terms? | Conflict check preparation
R: Have you spoken to an insurer or another lawyer? | Status and competition
R: Where did it happen? | Jurisdiction
P: Keep it short and gentle. Do not ask for the full story, medical records, photos of injuries or details that belong in a confidential meeting. For non-personal-injury enquiries, route appropriately. See [legal intake automation and conflict checks](/blog/legal-intake-automation-conflict-checks).
H2[urgency-signals]: Urgency signals
TABLE:
H: Signal | Action
R: Incident within days | Priority call within the hour
R: Deadline approaching | Urgent alert to a senior case handler
R: Serious injury or hospitalisation | Empathetic priority, and a call from an experienced person
R: Insurer pressing for a statement or settlement | Priority: the firm may need to advise quickly
R: Fatality or vulnerable client | Immediate human contact
P: The assistant should not draw legal conclusions from these signals; it should flag them so a person can.
H2[alerts-and-routing]: Alerts and routing
UL:
- Instant text and email to the on-call case manager for priority leads.
- A shared inbox for the rest, reviewed within an hour.
- Round-robin among available handlers, with a fallback.
- A summary of what the visitor said, with the conversation attached.
P: Measure the time from enquiry to first human contact, and target minutes for priority leads. See [instant lead routing in chat](/blog/instant-lead-routing-chat) and [webhook automations](/blog/webhook-automations-zapier-make-chat-events) for texting the on-call phone.
H2[rules-on-advertising-and-solicitation]: Rules on advertising and solicitation
UL:
- Do not make guarantees about results or compensation.
- Do not use language that pressures or exploits vulnerable people.
- Follow rules on how and when you may contact people who have not contacted you.
- State your status and how you are paid where required.
- Include required disclaimers, as your regulator specifies.
- Do not use testimonials or statistics in ways your rules forbid.
P: Have your ethics partner approve everything the assistant says, and review transcripts regularly for wording that drifts.
H2[set-expectations-honestly]: Set expectations honestly
P: Tell people what will happen next and when. 'A case manager will call you within 30 minutes during office hours, or by 9:00 a.m. tomorrow' is better than a vague 'we will be in touch'. If your first call is free and there is no fee unless you win, and that is accurate and permitted, state it as your rules allow. Never promise a result.
H2[a-sample-conversation]: A sample conversation
TABLE:
H: Speaker | Message
R: Visitor | I was hit by a car yesterday while crossing the road. My leg is in a cast.
R: Assistant | I am so sorry, I hope you are being looked after. I am an AI assistant and cannot give legal advice. If you have any new or worsening symptoms, please get medical help straight away. I would like one of our case managers to call you. What is your name and the best number, and is there a good time today?
R: Visitor | Sam, 555-0177, any time.
R: Assistant | Thank you, Sam. I have marked this as a priority and a case manager will call you within 30 minutes. Please do not worry about details for now; they will listen and explain the next steps.
H2[after-the-first-call]: After the first call
OL:
- Log the outcome and next steps in your matter system.
- Send a written summary and engagement information as your rules require.
- Follow up if the client has not signed within an agreed time, respectfully.
- Ask for a review after the case concludes, if your rules permit.

H2[worked-example-a-weekend-response]: Worked example: a weekend response
P: A firm receives enquiries on Saturday night. Previously they waited until Monday. Now the assistant replies instantly with the empathetic message, checks for medical needs and captures the visitor's name and number. Priority leads, incidents within a few days or involving hospitalisation, text the on-call case manager, who calls within thirty minutes on Sunday morning. Others are queued for Monday, and the assistant tells them so honestly. Over a quarter the firm signs several clients it would probably have lost, and hears from callers that being answered on a Saturday night mattered to them. The improvement came from a clear process, an on-call rota and honest expectations, not from the software alone.
H2[rota-and-process-checklist]: Rota and process checklist
UL:
- Named on-call case manager for nights and weekends, with a backup.
- A rule for who calls whom and how quickly.
- A script for the first call: listen, check on wellbeing, explain the next steps.
- A record of every call and outcome.
- A weekly review of response times and missed alerts.
P: The assistant provides speed; people provide care. The firms that do this well train their case managers to treat the first call as a conversation with someone in distress, not a sales call.
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Letting the assistant say anything about the strength of the case.
- Asking for detailed accounts of the accident in chat.
- Ignoring rules on solicitation.
- Sending alerts to an inbox nobody watches on weekends.
- Failing to check for immediate medical needs.
- Promising results or timings the firm cannot guarantee.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: Can a chatbot respond to personal injury enquiries?
A: Yes, for empathetic first contact, safety wording, basic triage and alerting your team, as long as it gives no advice and follows advertising rules.
Q: How fast should a firm respond?
A: As fast as possible; aim for minutes for priority leads and an instant automated reply at any hour.
Q: What should the chatbot say about injuries?
A: Express concern and encourage medical care if needed. It should never assess severity.
Q: Can it promise compensation?
A: No. It must not promise outcomes or amounts.
Q: What should it collect?
A: Name, contact details, type and date of incident and whether they have had medical treatment, kept brief.
Q: Who approves the wording?
A: Your ethics or compliance partner, with regular transcript reviews.
