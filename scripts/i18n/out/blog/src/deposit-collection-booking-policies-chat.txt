P: A deposit changes the meaning of a booking. Without one, a booking is a casual intention that costs the customer nothing to abandon. With one, it is a commitment with something at stake, and both sides take it seriously. Deposits, cancellation fees and late policies are among the most effective ways to reduce no-shows, and among the most sensitive: badly explained, they upset customers and cause disputes; well explained, they are accepted as fair. A chat is a good place to explain policies because visitors can ask questions and get the same, accurate answer every time. This guide explains when deposits help, how to write policies fairly, how to word them for chat, how to handle refunds, how the assistant should and should not talk about them, and how payment fits with a booking tool that may not take payments itself.
NOTE: Rules on deposits, cancellation fees and consumer rights vary by country, state and profession. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the rules that apply to you, and make sure your terms are clear and agreed before the booking.
H2[when-deposits-help]: When deposits help
TABLE:
H: Situation | Why a deposit helps
R: Long or expensive services | A missed slot costs real money
R: High no-show history | Commitment reduces casual booking
R: Peak times and popular slots | Protects scarce capacity
R: Custom or prepared work | Time and materials are committed in advance
R: New customers | You have no relationship yet
P: They help less, and can deter customers, for cheap, quick services and for regulars with a good record. Many businesses apply deposits selectively.
H2[the-building-blocks-of-a-policy]: The building blocks of a policy
TABLE:
H: Element | Question to answer
R: Deposit amount | How much, and is it a percentage or a fixed sum?
R: What it covers | Is it deducted from the final price or separate?
R: When it is taken | At booking or later?
R: Cancellation window | Until when can the customer cancel free of charge?
R: What happens after the window | Is the deposit kept? Partly?
R: Lateness | How late is too late, and what then?
R: Emergencies | How do you treat genuine exceptions?
R: Rescheduling | Is one free change allowed?
P: Answer every one in a sentence, and write them down. Vague policies cause the arguments they are meant to prevent.
H2[wording-that-customers-accept]: Wording that customers accept
TABLE:
H: Harsh | Fair
R: No refunds. | You can change or cancel free of charge up to 24 hours before. After that we may keep your deposit, because the time is reserved for you.
R: Deposits are non-negotiable. | We ask for a small deposit to secure your time. It comes off your final bill.
R: You will be charged if you do not show. | If you cannot make it, please let us know. If we hear from you inside 24 hours, we may keep your deposit, but tell us if something unexpected happened and we will do our best to help.
R: Late clients will be turned away. | If you are more than 15 minutes late we may need to shorten your service or rebook, so please message us if you are running behind.
UL:
- Use plain words and a warm tone.
- Explain the reason: the time is reserved for you.
- Offer a route for genuine emergencies.
- Show it before payment and again in the confirmation.
H2[how-the-assistant-should-talk-about-policies]: How the assistant should talk about policies
UL:
- State the policy exactly as written; never improvise a fee or exception.
- If asked for an exception, say that a team member will consider it, and hand over.
- Explain the reason gently.
- Do not threaten or pressure.
- If a visitor disputes a charge, hand over to a person.
P: Load the policy into the knowledge base as a short, clearly titled document, and test the assistant with questions such as 'What if I cancel tomorrow?' and 'Can I get my deposit back?'
H2[collecting-the-deposit]: Collecting the deposit
P: Crescelo's booking does not take payments or deposits itself. It offers slots, confirms bookings by email with a private link to change or cancel, and sends reminders. If you use deposits, take them through your own payment process, such as a payment link from your payment provider, an invoice or your point-of-sale system, and reference the policy in the confirmation. Some businesses collect the deposit when the customer arrives at a consultation instead of at the booking. Whichever you choose, make it clear before the booking is confirmed.
TABLE:
H: Method | Pros | Cons
R: Payment link sent after booking | Simple; works with any provider | Manual step; customer may not pay
R: Payment collected on a booking page | Automatic | Requires a booking tool with payments
R: Invoice | Familiar for professional services | Slower
R: Card held on file | No upfront cost to the customer | Needs a provider that supports it and clear consent

H2[refunds-and-exceptions]: Refunds and exceptions
OL:
- Decide in advance what qualifies as an exception: illness, emergency, bereavement.
- Ask for a short explanation, not proof, unless the amount is large.
- Apply rules consistently but with kindness.
- Record the reason and the decision.
- Refund promptly when you agree to; late refunds create disputes.
P: A rigid policy applied without judgement loses good customers. The most successful businesses combine clear rules with visible flexibility.
H2[legal-and-fairness-cautions]: Legal and fairness cautions
UL:
- Terms must be clear and agreed before the customer is bound.
- Consumer protection rules may limit fees or require refunds in some circumstances.
- Deposits in regulated professions may be restricted.
- Do not use hidden or surprise fees.
- Keep your website, chat and confirmation consistent.
P: If in doubt, take advice. See [effective cancellation policies for service businesses](/blog/effective-cancellation-policies-service-businesses).

H2[worked-example-a-colour-salon-introduces-deposits]: Worked example: a colour salon introduces deposits
P: A salon loses about eight colour appointments a month to no-shows, roughly $1,000 in lost revenue. It introduces a $30 deposit for colour services only, deducted from the final bill, with free changes up to 24 hours before. It writes the policy in the confirmation, adds it to the assistant's documents and sends payment links after booking. In the first two months, no-shows drop to two a month, and a handful of customers decline to book because of the deposit. The owner judges the trade worth it, waives the deposit for long-standing regulars and keeps a note of every exception. The lesson is that a deposit works best with clear wording, a visible route for genuine emergencies and a willingness to use discretion.

H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: Should I charge a deposit?
A: For long or expensive services, peak times and no-show-prone situations, often yes. For cheap, quick services, it may deter more than it helps.
Q: How do I word a cancellation policy?
A: Plainly and warmly: explain the window, what happens after it and that genuine emergencies are considered.
Q: Does Crescelo take deposits?
A: No. Its booking sends confirmations and reminders; take deposits through your own payment process.
Q: Can the assistant grant exceptions?
A: No. It states the policy and passes exception requests to a person.
Q: Are deposits legal?
A: Usually, if terms are clear and agreed in advance, but rules vary. Check the law where you operate.
Q: How do I handle refunds?
A: Decide what qualifies, apply rules consistently with kindness and refund promptly when agreed.
