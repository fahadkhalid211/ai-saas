P: Traditional live chat puts a person on the other end of the chat bubble. An AI assistant puts software there, reading your documents and replying instantly, with a person available when needed. Both promise the same thing, a visitor gets an answer without picking up the phone, but they get there in opposite ways, with different costs, strengths and risks. This guide compares them across the things a small business cares about, shows a worked cost example and explains how many businesses combine the two so each does what it does best.
P: We build Crescelo, an AI assistant, so read this knowing our interest. We say where live chat is the better tool, because sometimes it is.
H2[what-each-one-is]: What each one is
H3: Traditional live chat
P: A chat window connected to an inbox where human agents reply in real time. Good products add canned replies, visitor information, routing, ticketing and reports. The quality of the experience depends entirely on who is online and how fast they type. Some products now add AI features, but the core model is people answering people.
H3: An AI assistant
P: A chat window connected to a knowledge base and a language model. It answers from your own documents, books appointments, captures leads and hands over to your team when it should. It is available at all hours and replies in seconds. A person can join any conversation. See [what an AI website assistant is](/blog/ai-website-assistant) for the mechanics.

H2[where-live-chat-is-the-better-tool]: Where live chat is the better tool
UL:
- Visitors ask complex or high-value questions that need judgement and empathy.
- Your sales process is consultative and a personal conversation closes deals.
- You have staff whose main job is chat and who can keep response times low.
- The questions are so varied that no documentation covers them.
P: If that describes you, live chat with good agents is hard to beat. The mistake is to assume every enquiry deserves that level of attention.
H2[where-an-ai-assistant-is-the-better-tool]: Where an AI assistant is the better tool
UL:
- Most questions are repeatable: prices, hours, services, availability, policies.
- Enquiries arrive outside the hours you can staff.
- You want bookings and leads captured without waiting for someone.
- Your team is small and cannot watch a chat all day.
- You want to know what customers ask and where your website fails to answer.
P: For a salon, clinic, contractor or agency with a small team, this is usually most of the volume.

H2[handover-the-feature-that-makes-the-combination-work]: Handover: the feature that makes the combination work
P: The strongest setups are not either-or. The assistant handles the first layer, and the visitor can ask for a person at any time. When they do, your team is notified with the whole conversation and can take over from their phone or desk. The visitor does not repeat anything. In Crescelo this is the [human handover feature](/product/human-handover). See [live human handover flow design](/blog/live-human-handover-flow-design) for how to design it well.
OL:
- The visitor asks for a person, or the assistant recognises it should hand over.
- Your team gets an alert with the conversation attached.
- A person joins and replies as themselves.
- When done, the assistant can resume or the conversation is closed and recorded.
H2[quality-and-trust]: Quality and trust
P: Visitors accept AI when it is fast, honest about being AI and easy to escape. They reject it when it traps them or invents answers. Ground the assistant in your documents, instruct it to say when it does not know, and test it with questions your content cannot answer. See [how to stop a chatbot from making things up](/blog/grounded-ai-chatbot-hallucinations). Live chat has its own quality risk, which is inconsistency: the same question answered differently by two agents.

TABLE:

R: Bookings from chat | Manual or via link | Direct

H2[how-to-decide]: How to decide
OL:
- Count your chats and enquiries per month and the hours they arrive.
- Sample fifty and mark which are routine and which need a person.
- Estimate the enquiries lost outside staffed hours.
- If most are routine, start with an assistant on the first line.
- If most need judgement, keep live chat and add automation for the simple ones.
- Review after a month using response time, bookings and cost per chat.
H2[migrating-from-live-chat-to-an-assistant]: Migrating from live chat to an assistant
P: You do not have to turn live chat off. Export your best canned replies and past transcripts, turn them into documents, load them into the assistant and test with real past questions. Run both for a few weeks, with the assistant on the first line and agents receiving handovers. When answers are reliable, reduce staffed hours to when volume justifies them.
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Buying live chat and not staffing it.
- Buying an assistant and never reviewing conversations.
- Hiding the way to reach a person.
- Comparing only the software price.
- Letting the assistant answer sensitive topics without rules for handover.
- Judging either tool on volume instead of bookings and leads.

H2[signs-it-is-time-to-add-an-assistant-to-live-chat]: Signs it is time to add an assistant to live chat
UL:
- Chats often wait more than a minute for a reply.
- Your chat is offline more hours than it is online.
- Agents report that most chats are the same few questions.
- Evening and weekend enquiries go unanswered.
- You cannot tell which questions your website fails to answer.
- You pay per seat for people who spend most of their time on other work.
H2[signs-live-chat-should-stay-the-centre]: Signs live chat should stay the centre
UL:
- Most conversations are consultative sales where the agent's judgement matters.
- You already have well-staffed hours matching your visitors' hours.
- Your customers expect a named person.
- Your team enjoys and is good at chat.
P: Even then, an assistant on the edges, for after-hours and simple questions, tends to help without displacing anyone.
H2[questions-to-ask-a-live-chat-vendor]: Questions to ask a live chat vendor
OL:
- What does the price become when I add a second and third agent?
- Are canned replies, routing and reports included or extra?
- What automation exists, and does it answer from my own documents?
- Can visitors book an appointment without leaving the chat?
- What happens to chats when nobody is online?
- Can I export transcripts and visitor data if I leave?
P: Ask an assistant vendor the equivalents: what happens when it does not know, how do I take over, and what does it cost at twice my volume. Comparing the answers side by side is more informative than comparing feature lists.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: Is an AI assistant better than live chat?
A: For routine questions, bookings and out-of-hours coverage, usually yes. For complex, emotional or high-value conversations, a person is better. Many businesses use both.
Q: Can an AI assistant replace live chat agents?
A: It can take the routine load so agents handle fewer, more valuable conversations. It does not remove the need for people in sensitive or complex cases.
Q: What does live chat really cost?
A: The subscription is small; the main cost is staff time and missed enquiries outside coverage hours.
Q: Can visitors talk to a person through an AI assistant?
A: Yes. In Crescelo a visitor can ask for a person at any time, and your team is notified with the whole conversation.
Q: Which is faster to set up?
A: Both take hours. Live chat needs staffing plans; an assistant needs good content and testing.
Q: Can I keep my existing live chat?
A: Yes. You can run the assistant on the first line and route to your live chat team, or replace live chat gradually.
