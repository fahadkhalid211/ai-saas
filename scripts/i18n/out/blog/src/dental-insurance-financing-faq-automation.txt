MOD: 2026-09-27
P: 'Do you take my insurance?' may be the most common question a dental practice hears, followed closely by 'how much will it cost?' and 'do you offer payment plans?' These questions arrive by phone, by email and in the website chat, often while the front desk is busy with patients. They are also risky to answer casually. Coverage depends on the individual's plan, and a wrong answer can lead to a surprise bill, an angry patient and a complaint. Automating these answers is worthwhile, as long as the assistant states what the practice knows, avoids promises about coverage and hands over when the question needs a person. This guide explains how to write the content, what the assistant should and should not say, how to present fees and payment options, and how to test the answers.
NOTE: Insurance and payment rules differ by country, and this is general information, not legal or financial advice. Have your practice manager or adviser review the wording, and keep it current.
H2[what-patients-actually-ask]: What patients actually ask
TABLE:
H: Question | What the practice can safely say
R: Do you take my insurance? | Which insurers or networks you work with, and that coverage depends on the plan and must be confirmed with the insurer
R: What does my plan cover? | Nothing specific: refer them to their insurer, and offer that the team can help check benefits at booking
R: How much is a first visit without insurance? | Your published self-pay fee
R: How much for a crown, implant or braces? | A published range if you have one, or that treatment plans need an examination first
R: Do you offer payment plans? | The options you provide and how to apply
R: Do you accept card, cash, health savings accounts? | The accepted methods
R: Can I get a quote in advance? | How to request an estimate after an assessment
P: Notice the pattern: state facts you control, and refer everything that depends on an individual plan or clinical assessment.
H2[write-the-source-documents-first]: Write the source documents first
P: The assistant can only be as good as the documents. Prepare a short set.
OL:
- **Insurance FAQ:** the list of insurers or networks you are in, how the practice handles claims (direct billing or patient reimbursement) and how patients can check their benefits.
- **Fee guide:** self-pay fees for common services, with dates so they can be kept current.
- **Payment options:** accepted methods, payment plans, financing partners and how to apply.
- **Estimates policy:** when and how you provide written estimates and what they depend on.
- **Cancellation and billing policy:** what happens with late cancellations and unpaid balances.
P: Keep each document short and clearly titled, for example 'Which insurance do you accept?' Put the answer near the question, and date anything that changes.
H2[wording-that-avoids-overpromising]: Wording that avoids overpromising
TABLE:
H: Risky | Safer
R: Yes, your plan covers that. | We work with your insurer, but coverage depends on your specific plan. We can help check your benefits when you book, and we recommend confirming with your insurer.
R: It will cost about $200. | Our fee for a check-up and cleaning without insurance is $150. With insurance, your cost depends on your plan.
R: You will not have to pay anything. | Many plans cover preventive visits in full, but your plan determines that. We will give you an estimate before treatment.
R: Do not worry about the price. | I understand cost is a concern. We offer payment plans, and we can talk through options at your visit.
UL:
- Never state what an individual plan will cover.
- Never guarantee a final cost before an examination.
- Always give a route to a person for individual benefit questions.
- Give figures only if you publish them and keep them current.
H2[present-fees-and-financing-clearly]: Present fees and financing clearly
P: Transparent pricing builds trust and filters out mismatched enquiries. If you publish fees, present them simply: service, price, what it includes. For larger treatments, give ranges and explain what affects the cost. For financing, explain the options and where to find details, and avoid quoting interest rates or terms that belong to a third-party lender unless you have current, approved wording. Say that financing is subject to approval where that is the case.
H2[handing-over-the-tricky-cases]: Handing over the tricky cases
UL:
- Questions about a specific plan's benefits or deductibles.
- Disputes about a bill or a claim.
- Complex treatment plans and estimates.
- Payment difficulties and hardship requests.
- Anything the documents do not cover.
P: The assistant should say what it can, then offer to pass the question to the billing or front desk team and capture the patient's contact details. See [live human handover flow design](/blog/live-human-handover-flow-design).
H2[privacy-keep-insurance-identifiers-out-of-the-chat]: Privacy: keep insurance identifiers out of the chat
P: Ask patients not to type member numbers, group numbers or dates of birth in an open chat. Say where to provide them: at booking through a secure form, by phone or at the visit. Include a short notice in the chat. See [new patient intake for dental practices](/blog/dental-new-patient-intake-automation) and [HIPAA considerations for dental chatbots](/blog/hipaa-compliance-dental-chatbots).
H2[test-the-answers]: Test the answers
TABLE:
H: Test question | A good answer
R: Do you take Delta Dental? | States your position and that coverage depends on the plan; offers to check benefits
R: Will insurance cover my crown? | Does not promise; explains the next step
R: How much is a cleaning if I have no insurance? | The published self-pay fee
R: Can I pay in instalments? | Describes your payment options
R: I was billed the wrong amount. | Empathy and a handover to the billing team
R: What is the price of a smile makeover? | Explains it needs a consultation; offers to book one
P: Run these and a dozen more before launch, and rerun them whenever fees or insurers change. See [the questions every service business chatbot should answer well](/blog/essential-chatbot-questions-service-business).
H2[keep-it-current]: Keep it current
UL:
- Put a review date on every fee and insurance document.
- Update the assistant's knowledge on the same day you change a fee or add or drop an insurer.
- Delete old versions so they cannot conflict.
- Review conversations weekly to find questions you did not expect; see [how to review your chatbot's conversations](/blog/audit-website-chatbot-conversations).
H2[a-sample-conversation]: A sample conversation
TABLE:
H: Speaker | Message
R: Patient | Do you take Delta Dental, and how much is a cleaning?
R: Assistant | Yes, we work with Delta Dental. Coverage depends on your specific plan, so we recommend confirming your benefits with Delta, and we can help check them when you book. Our self-pay fee for a check-up and cleaning is $150. Would you like to see available times?
R: Patient | Will my plan cover all of it?
R: Assistant | Many plans cover preventive visits in large part, but I cannot say what yours covers. Your insurer can tell you, and our team can help check your benefits before your visit. If you like, I can take your details so they can do that.
P: The assistant stated what the practice knows, gave a published fee, avoided promising coverage and offered a person. That is the pattern to aim for.
H2[a-small-template-for-your-insurance-page]: A small template for your insurance page
UL:
- A plain-language sentence: 'We work with the following insurers: ...' with a date.
- A note that coverage depends on the individual plan and that patients should confirm benefits.
- How your team can help check benefits, and what they need from the patient.
- What happens if the plan pays less than expected.
- Your self-pay fees and payment options.
- How to contact the billing team.
P: A page like this serves patients and the assistant, and it saves the front desk from typing the same answer many times a week.
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Listing insurers that the practice no longer works with.
- Letting the assistant say 'yes, that is covered'.
- Quoting old fees after a price change.
- Leaving financing details unclear, so patients expect terms you do not offer.
- Not giving billing questions a person to talk to.
H2[a-quarterly-review]: A quarterly review
P: Once a quarter, sit with your billing lead and read the last fifty insurance and payment conversations. Note the questions you did not expect, the answers that were too vague and any complaint that started in the chat. Update the documents, retest the sample questions and record what you changed. Ten quiet minutes here prevent most billing surprises.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: Can a chatbot answer dental insurance questions?
A: It can share which insurers you work with and your fees, and explain that coverage depends on the plan. It should not promise coverage and should hand individual benefit questions to your team.
Q: Should the chatbot quote prices for treatments?
A: Only for services with published fees. For larger treatments, explain that a consultation is needed and offer to book one.
Q: How do I stop the chatbot promising coverage?
A: Write the documents and the assistant's instructions so that it never states what a plan covers, and test with questions that tempt it to.
Q: Can it explain payment plans?
A: Yes, from your approved documents, including where to apply and that financing is subject to approval.
Q: Should patients type their insurance number in the chat?
A: No. Direct them to a secure form, the phone or the visit.
Q: How often should I update the information?
A: Whenever fees or insurers change, and review it at least quarterly.
