P: A property enquiry has a short shelf life. The visitor is looking at a listing on their phone, they like it, and they want to see it. If they have to fill in a form and wait for a reply, they will send the same message to three other agents, and the first to offer a real time often gets the viewing. Chat can close that gap: it answers questions about the listing and offers times in the same conversation, at any hour. This guide explains how agents and property managers can set it up, what to ask before offering a slot, how to handle timing, travel and access, how to reduce no-shows, how to protect the safety of agents and how to follow up so viewings become offers.
H2[what-a-viewing-enquiry-looks-like]: What a viewing enquiry looks like
TABLE:
H: Visitor | Typical message | What helps
R: Buyer | Is this still available? Can I see it this weekend? | Status, price, a real slot
R: Renter | Is it pet friendly? When can I move in? Can I view tomorrow evening? | Tenancy terms, availability, viewing time
R: Investor | What is the yield and can I see it with my agent? | Facts you publish, a route to a person
R: Seller | Can someone value my home? | A valuation appointment rather than a viewing
P: The assistant answers from the listing document and offers a time when the visitor is ready. Keep listing information current; a sold property still shown as available is the fastest way to lose trust.
H2[what-to-ask-before-offering-a-slot]: What to ask before offering a slot
OL:
- Which property and, if relevant, which unit or address.
- Whether they are a buyer, renter or investor, because the next steps differ.
- Their preferred day and time window.
- How many people will attend.
- Name and a mobile number to confirm.
- For buyers, whether they have finance arranged or a property to sell; ask gently and only if you need it.
P: Three questions are usually enough to book. The rest can wait for the viewing. See [buyer versus seller lead qualification](/blog/buyer-vs-seller-lead-qualification-chat) for how to qualify without interrogating.
H2[slot-rules-that-keep-agents-sane]: Slot rules that keep agents sane
TABLE:
H: Rule | Why
R: Viewing windows by agent and area | Agents cannot be in two places at once
R: Travel time between viewings | Back-to-back across town fails
R: Viewing length | 15 to 30 minutes is typical; longer for larger properties
R: Notice period | Access may need tenants' consent or a key
R: Daylight and safety limits | Avoid late viewings alone
R: Open house times | Group visits at fixed times

H2[confirmations-that-reduce-no-shows]: Confirmations that reduce no-shows
UL:
- Address, meeting point and how to find the entrance.
- The agent's name and mobile number.
- Parking and transport notes.
- What to bring: identification for a rental, if required.
- A link to change or cancel.
- A reminder the day before and a short message the morning of the viewing.
P: A confirmation with a change link matters most: many missed viewings are people who could not find how to move the time. Crescelo sends confirmations and email reminders with a private link to reschedule or cancel.
H2[open-houses-and-group-viewings]: Open houses and group viewings
P: For open houses, the chat can collect registrations, state the time window and give parking and access details. Registration lets you follow up with everyone who came, and can support safety by knowing who is expected. Group viewings need capacity control; if your booking tool does not manage group capacity, use a registration lead flow instead of individual slots. See [open house registration workflows](/blog/open-house-registration-workflows-chat).
H2[agent-safety]: Agent safety
UL:
- Record the name and phone number of every visitor before a viewing.
- Prefer daylight and, for solo viewings, share the schedule with the office.
- Verify identity where your company requires it.
- Never share the agent's home details or personal numbers.
- Have a check-in routine, such as a text after each viewing.
P: Collecting contact details in the chat supports this, and it gives you a record if something goes wrong.
H2[after-the-viewing]: After the viewing
OL:
- Send a thank-you message within a few hours, with the listing link and next steps.
- Ask what they thought and whether they would like to see similar properties.
- For interested buyers, explain how to make an offer.
- For renters, explain the application process.
- Record feedback for the seller or landlord.
P: The chat can prompt the visitor to reply with feedback, and pass the reply to the agent. Keep the tone helpful, not pushy.
H2[what-the-assistant-should-not-do]: What the assistant should not do
UL:
- Make claims about the property that are not in the listing.
- Negotiate price or promise the seller will accept an offer.
- Give legal, mortgage or investment advice.
- Guarantee availability.
- Share private information about sellers or tenants.
P: Have it say 'I will pass that to the agent' when the question goes beyond the listing.

H2[worked-example-a-busy-saturday-for-a-lettings-agency]: Worked example: a busy Saturday for a lettings agency
P: A lettings agency has eight flats to show on Saturday and three agents. The week before, the assistant is told each agent's viewing hours and the flat addresses, and slots are set at twenty minutes. By Thursday, thirty-one viewings are booked, twelve of them after 6 p.m. the evening before. Each visitor receives a confirmation with the address, the agent's name and a change link, and a reminder on Friday. On Saturday morning two visitors cancel using the link, which frees their slots for late enquirers. Agents arrive to a schedule that is realistic, with contact details already collected. The agency records four no-shows, down from around ten in a typical weekend. The improvement came from clear confirmations and easy changes, not from any pressure on tenants.

H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Offering viewing slots without travel time between properties.
- Leaving sold or let properties in the listing documents.
- Not collecting a phone number before a viewing.
- Forgetting to send reminders.
- Letting agents change times without informing visitors.
- Not following up after viewings.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: Can a chatbot book property viewings?
A: Yes. It answers listing questions and offers real slots, with a confirmation and reminders.
Q: How do I stop viewing no-shows?
A: Send a clear confirmation with a change link, remind the day before and the morning of, and confirm contact details at booking.
Q: Can it handle several agents?
A: With separate booking links per agent through Calendly or Cal.com, or a shared pool you manage. Crescelo's built-in picker uses one slot length and your opening hours.
Q: What about open houses?
A: Use the chat to collect registrations and share the time window and access details.
Q: Should it verify visitors?
A: Collect name and phone number before every viewing, and follow your company's safety policy.
Q: Can it give mortgage advice?
A: No. It can explain your process and refer to a broker.
