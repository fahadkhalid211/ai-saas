P: You run ads, post on social media, appear in search results, hand out flyers and get referrals. A lead arrives through the chat on your website. Which of those efforts earned it? Working that out is called attribution, and it matters because it tells you where to spend the next hour or the next pound. Big companies use complicated models and dedicated tools. A small business can get most of the value from three habits: asking people how they found you, tagging links so your analytics can see them and keeping a simple lead sheet that records the source. This guide explains what attribution is, why it is imperfect, how to collect source information from chat leads, how to use UTM tags, how to compare channels fairly, which simple models to use and what to avoid. It is honest about Crescelo's role: it captures leads and the conversations behind them, and lets you choose form fields, so you can ask 'How did you hear about us?', but it does not automatically track advertising campaigns or marketing sources for you.
H2[why-attribution-is-hard]: Why attribution is hard
UL:
- Customers use several touchpoints: they see an ad, search your name, read a review and then chat.
- Browsers and privacy tools limit tracking.
- Word of mouth and offline sources leave no digital trail.
- The last click is not necessarily the reason they chose you.
P: Perfect attribution does not exist. The goal is a good enough picture to decide where to invest.
H2[method-1-just-ask]: Method 1: just ask
P: The simplest and most useful method is to ask the customer. Add a field to your lead form, or have the assistant ask naturally after a helpful answer: 'By the way, how did you hear about us?' Offer common options plus 'other'.
TABLE:
H: Options to offer | Notes
R: Google search | Includes maps
R: Social media (which one?) | Ask which platform if you use several
R: A friend or family member | Referral
R: An advert | Where?
R: A flyer or local paper | Offline
R: Saw you in person | Offline
R: Other | Free text
P: Self-reported sources are subjective, but they capture what analytics cannot, such as recommendations and offline exposure. Crescelo lets you choose lead form fields, so you can add this question and see the answer with each lead.
H2[method-2-tag-your-links-with-utm-parameters]: Method 2: tag your links with UTM parameters
P: UTM parameters are labels added to a link so analytics tools can see where a visit came from. For links you control, such as ads, newsletters, social posts and your bio link, add them.
TABLE:
H: Parameter | Meaning | Example
R: utm_source | Where the link was placed | instagram
R: utm_medium | The type of channel | social, email, cpc
R: utm_campaign | The campaign or promotion | spring-offer
P: Example: yoursite.com/book?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=spring-offer. Your website analytics then groups visits by these labels. Keep naming consistent: lowercase, no spaces. Note that Crescelo does not read UTM tags into leads itself, so pair this with the 'how did you hear' question, or compare chat volume with visits from each source in your analytics.
H2[method-3-give-channels-their-own-landing-pages]: Method 3: give channels their own landing pages
P: A dedicated page for each major channel, such as a hosted page for your Instagram bio link, lets you count visits and chats per channel. Crescelo's hosted pages let you create a page with its own assistant and link; using a separate page per channel makes the source clear from where the visitor started. See [Instagram and TikTok bio links that lead straight to booking](/blog/social-bio-link-in-chat-booking).
H2[method-4-a-lead-sheet]: Method 4: a lead sheet
TABLE:
H: Column | Purpose
R: Date | When the lead arrived
R: Name and contact | Who they are
R: Service | What they wanted
R: Self-reported source | From the form question
R: Tagged source | If known from analytics or the landing page
R: Outcome | Won, lost, no reply
R: Value | The revenue if won
P: Crescelo can send leads to Google Sheets, which makes this straightforward. Add the outcome and value columns yourself as jobs are won.
H2[simple-attribution-models]: Simple attribution models
TABLE:
H: Model | How it works | Use when
R: First touch | Credit the first known source | You want to know what brings new people in
R: Last touch | Credit the source just before the lead | You want to know what closes
R: Self-reported | Credit what the customer says | You are a small business with limited tracking
R: Split | Share credit between sources | You have data on several touchpoints
P: For most small businesses, the self-reported source combined with tagged links is enough. Do not chase complex models before you have a reliable lead sheet.
H2[compare-channels-fairly]: Compare channels fairly
UL:
- Divide leads by cost: cost per lead for each paid channel.
- Go further and compute cost per won job, using the outcome column.
- Consider lead quality: a channel with fewer, better leads may be more valuable.
- Allow for time: some channels take weeks to show results.
- Value free channels: search visibility and referrals cost time, not ad spend.
TABLE:
H: Channel | Leads | Won | Spend | Cost per won job
R: Search ads | 20 | 5 | $400 | $80
R: Instagram | 12 | 2 | $100 | $50
R: Referrals | 8 | 6 | $0 | $0 (but time)
P: The numbers above are illustrative. Note how the lead count alone would hide the fact that referrals convert best.
H2[privacy-and-consent]: Privacy and consent
UL:
- Analytics and advertising cookies need consent in many places.
- Explain why you ask how someone found you; it is optional.
- Do not collect more than you need.
- See [privacy law for lead capture](/blog/chatbot-lead-capture-gdpr-ccpa-compliance).
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Trusting last click alone.
- Ignoring offline and referral sources.
- Inconsistent UTM names.
- Never recording outcomes, so you count leads but not customers.
- Assuming the platform tracks campaigns when it does not.
- Over-engineering before you have data.
H2[a-worked-example]: A worked example
P: A cleaning company asks each chat lead 'How did you hear about us?' and records the answer in a Google Sheet. After three months, the sheet shows 30 leads from search, 18 from social media, 11 from friends and 6 from flyers. Recording the outcome shows that referrals were won far more often than the others, while social leads mostly asked for prices and never replied. The owner decides to add a small thank-you offer for customers who recommend a friend, cut back on social advertising and improve the services page for search visitors. None of this needed special software, only a form question and a spreadsheet kept up to date.
TABLE:
H: Source | Leads | Won | Win rate
R: Search | 30 | 9 | 30%
R: Social media | 18 | 2 | 11%
R: Friend or family | 11 | 8 | 73%
R: Flyer | 6 | 1 | 17%
P: These are illustrative numbers. Your own table will look different, and that is the point: it shows where your next hour or pound is best spent.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: How do I know where my chat leads come from?
A: Ask them with a lead form question, tag your links with UTM parameters and keep a lead sheet that records the source and outcome.
Q: Does Crescelo track marketing campaigns?
A: No. It captures leads and conversations and lets you add a 'how did you hear about us' field. Use your analytics for campaign tracking.
Q: What are UTM parameters?
A: Labels added to links so analytics tools can group visits by source, medium and campaign.
Q: Is asking customers reliable?
A: It is subjective but captures word of mouth and offline sources that tracking misses. Use it alongside tagged links.
Q: How do I compare channels?
A: Compare cost per won job, not just leads, and consider lead quality.
Q: Do I need special software?
A: No. A lead sheet, consistent link tags and a form question are enough to start.
