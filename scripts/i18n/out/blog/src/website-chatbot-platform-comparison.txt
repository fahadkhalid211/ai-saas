P: Searching for the best AI chatbot for your website returns dozens of products with similar promises. Feature lists look alike, demos always work, and pricing pages are built to be hard to compare. This guide gives you a method to compare platforms fairly for a service business, a scorecard you can copy, and the questions that expose weak products quickly. It does not crown a single winner, because the best choice depends on your business, and it is honest about where our own product, Crescelo, fits.
H2[start-with-what-you-need-not-the-feature-list]: Start with what you need, not the feature list
P: Before opening any product page, write down your job to be done. Different jobs need different tools.
TABLE:
H: If your main need is | Look for | Be careful about
R: Answering common questions from your documents | Strong grounding, source visibility, admits when it does not know | Products that answer from general knowledge
R: Booking appointments | Live calendar, slot locking, buffers, reminders, rescheduling | Chatbots that only collect a request and leave you to schedule
R: Capturing leads | Structured capture, instant alerts, CRM and webhook delivery | Leads trapped in the vendor's dashboard
R: Customer support with ticketing | Handover, inbox, integrations with a help desk | Bots with no way for a person to step in
R: Multi-channel presence | Website plus WhatsApp and Telegram in one inbox | Separate products per channel
H2[the-eight-things-to-compare]: The eight things to compare
H3: 1. Answer quality and grounding
P: This matters most. Load the same documents into each product and ask the same twenty questions, including three the documents cannot answer. Note which products admit ignorance and which invent something. Check whether you can see the source behind an answer. See [how to stop a chatbot from making things up](/blog/grounded-ai-chatbot-hallucinations).
H3: 2. Booking and calendar
P: If you book appointments, test a real booking, a time that is already taken and a reschedule. Ask how double bookings are prevented, how buffers and durations work and which calendars sync. Our guide to [AI appointment booking](/blog/ai-appointment-booking) lists what a trustworthy system must do.
H3: 3. Pricing model
P: There are three common models: a flat subscription with a conversation allowance, per-conversation or per-resolution pricing, and platform fees plus AI usage. Each behaves differently as you grow. Ask for the total price at your current volume and at three times that, including overage. Bring-your-own-key products let you pay the AI provider directly, which makes the AI cost visible; see [bring-your-own-key versus bundled pricing](/blog/byok-vs-bundled-ai-pricing-cost-comparison).
H3: 4. Integrations
P: Check the tools you already use: calendars, email, Slack, spreadsheets, CRM, WhatsApp and automation tools such as Zapier or Make. Ask whether integrations are included in your plan or cost extra. See [webhook automations with Zapier and Make](/blog/webhook-automations-zapier-make-chat-events) and [CRM integration](/blog/crm-integration-ai-chatbot-leads).
H3: 5. Handover to a person
P: You need to step into a conversation. Check for notifications, an inbox, history and the ability to reply as a person. See [live human handover flow design](/blog/live-human-handover-flow-design).
H3: 6. Security and privacy
P: Ask where data is stored, who the sub-processors are, how long conversations are kept, whether you can delete them, whether API keys are encrypted and whether the account supports two-factor authentication and roles. If you serve Europe or California, read the [GDPR checklist for website chatbots](/blog/website-chatbot-gdpr-checklist).
H3: 7. Performance and accessibility
P: Check that the widget loads without slowing your page and works with a keyboard and screen reader. See [performance of embedded chat widgets](/blog/performance-optimization-embedded-chat-widgets) and [chatbot accessibility](/blog/accessibility-wcag-compliance-website-chatbots).
H3: 8. Support and stability
P: Find out how to reach support, how fast they reply and whether the vendor shares a public status or roadmap. A young product with a clear roadmap can be a good bet; a product that hides what is and is not built is not.

H2[a-one-hour-test-protocol]: A one-hour test protocol
OL:
- Choose two or three finalists using the needs table above.
- Load the same set of your real documents into each.
- Ask the same twenty questions: ten common, five worded badly, three not in the documents, two that tempt it to over-promise.
- Run a real booking and a reschedule.
- Submit a test lead and watch how and where it arrives.
- Request a person and see what your team receives.
- Read the pricing page and calculate the total at three times your volume.
- Fill in the scorecard.
H2[red-flags]: Red flags
UL:
- Demos only with the vendor's own content.
- No way to see which passage produced an answer.
- Unclear pricing or 'contact us' for basic tiers when you are a small business.
- Leads only viewable inside the vendor's dashboard, with no export.
- Claims of guaranteed sales or fully replacing your staff.
- No information on data storage or sub-processors.

H2[hidden-costs-to-look-for]: Hidden costs to look for
UL:
- **Per-seat fees** for each team member who answers chats.
- **Overage charges** when conversations exceed the plan.
- **Add-ons** for booking, extra languages, white-labelling, WhatsApp or API access.
- **AI usage** that is billed separately or bundled with a margin.
- **Setup or onboarding fees.**
- **Storage limits** for documents, which force an upgrade as your content grows.
- **Annual-only discounts** that lock you in before you have tested the product.
H2[how-to-run-a-fair-trial]: How to run a fair trial
OL:
- Write down the twenty questions in advance so you cannot unconsciously favour one product.
- Use the same documents everywhere, including one messy PDF.
- Ask each question twice on different days, to see whether answers are consistent.
- Score answers as correct, partly correct, wrong or admitted ignorance. Admitted ignorance is better than wrong.
- Ask a colleague who did not choose the products to test them cold.
- Record how long setup took, because that is a real cost.
H2[questions-about-your-future-not-just-today]: Questions about your future, not just today
UL:
- Can I change the AI model or provider later?
- Can I export my content, conversations and leads?
- How often does the vendor ship improvements, and where can I see them?
- What happens to my data if I cancel?
- Is there a public roadmap that separates what is live from what is planned?

H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: What is the best AI chatbot for a small business website?
A: It depends on your main need. For appointment-based businesses, look for grounded answers, live calendar booking, lead capture and handover to a person. Compare a few products on your own documents.
Q: How much should an AI chatbot cost?
A: Small business plans commonly range from tens to a few hundred dollars a month, plus AI usage. Compare the total price at your current and three times your volume.
Q: Is a free AI chatbot good enough?
A: Free tiers are fine for testing. Check limits on conversations, storage and branding, and whether booking and lead delivery are included.
Q: Do I need my own AI key?
A: Not always, but bring-your-own-key products make AI costs transparent and let you switch models. Bundled products are simpler but add a margin.
Q: How do I stop a chatbot giving wrong answers?
A: Choose a product that answers from your documents and admits when it does not know, and test it with questions your content cannot answer.
Q: Can I switch chatbot later?
A: Usually yes, if you keep your source documents. Ask whether you can export conversations and leads.
