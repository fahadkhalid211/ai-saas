P: A virtual AI receptionist is software that does the first-line work of a front desk: greeting visitors, answering common questions, capturing contact details and booking appointments. It does not sit at a desk or take a lunch break, and it answers instantly at any hour. For a small business where the owner or a single team member also does everything else, that first layer is often what gets neglected, and where enquiries are lost.
P: This guide explains what a virtual AI receptionist actually does, how it differs from older tools, what to prepare, what it costs and how to keep the human touch. It covers the web-chat version, which is where Crescelo works today; phone answering is a separate channel that is under development at Crescelo, and we say where that matters.
H2[what-a-virtual-ai-receptionist-does]: What a virtual AI receptionist does
UL:
- **Greets and orients:** welcomes visitors and states what it can help with.
- **Answers questions** about services, prices, hours, location and policies from your documents.
- **Qualifies enquiries** by asking the questions you would ask, such as which service, when and where.
- **Books and reschedules appointments** into your calendar.
- **Captures leads** and sends them to your email, spreadsheet, Slack or other tools.
- **Escalates to a person** when a case needs one, with the conversation attached.
- **Records everything,** so you can see what customers ask and where the gaps are.
P: It is a receptionist for the routine layer. The judgement calls and the relationships still belong to your team.

H2[how-it-works-underneath]: How it works underneath
P: A virtual AI receptionist combines four parts. A knowledge base holds your documents. A retrieval step finds the passages that relate to a question. A language model writes the reply from those passages under instructions to answer only from them. And an action layer books appointments, records leads and notifies people. The action layer is ordinary software, which is why bookings can be exact even though the conversation is flexible. The mechanics are explained in [what an AI website assistant is](/blog/ai-website-assistant).
H2[training-it-on-your-business]: Training it on your business
P: There is no training in the machine-learning sense. You load your content and tell it how to behave. The quality of the result depends on three things.
OL:
- **Your service catalog:** clear names, prices, durations and what is included. See [training an AI receptionist on your service catalog](/blog/train-ai-receptionist-service-catalog).
- **Your policies:** cancellation, deposits, lateness, emergencies, refunds.
- **Your voice:** how formal, how friendly, what to avoid saying. See [brand voice alignment for an AI receptionist](/blog/brand-voice-alignment-ai-receptionist).
P: Test with the questions customers really ask, in different words and with typos, and fix each failure by improving the source content.
H2[business-hours-holidays-and-locations]: Business hours, holidays and locations
P: A receptionist that offers a 9 a.m. slot on a public holiday, or quotes the wrong branch's address, damages trust. Set your opening hours and holidays where the assistant can read them, and give each location its own information if you have more than one. See [business hours logic for AI chat](/blog/business-hours-logic-ai-chat) and [multi-location AI receptionists](/blog/multi-location-ai-receptionist).
H2[where-a-virtual-receptionist-should-hand-over]: Where a virtual receptionist should hand over
P: The best receptionists know their limits. Build clear rules for handing over to a person: complaints, requests for medical, legal or financial advice, urgent or emergency wording, anything involving a refund or dispute, and any visitor who asks for a person. The conversation should go with the handover so nobody has to repeat themselves. See [live human handover flow design](/blog/live-human-handover-flow-design) and [handling emergencies in automated chat](/blog/handling-emergencies-automated-chat).

H2[protecting-your-front-desk-team]: Protecting your front desk team
P: A common worry is that an AI receptionist replaces people. In practice it tends to remove the repetitive load that causes burnout, so staff spend their time on the conversations that need them. Front desks that are constantly interrupted make mistakes and lose patience. Our piece on [preventing front desk burnout with AI](/blog/prevent-front-desk-burnout-ai) explores this. Be open with your team about what the assistant will and will not do.

H2[setting-one-up-a-one-afternoon-plan]: Setting one up: a one-afternoon plan
OL:
- Gather your services, prices, hours and policies into short documents.
- Create the assistant and load the documents.
- Set the greeting and tone.
- Add your calendar and services if you want bookings.
- Test twenty questions, including three the content cannot answer.
- Set handover rules and notification emails.
- Publish the snippet on your site.
- Review the first week of conversations and fix gaps.
P: You can rehearse steps 2 to 5 with no account in the [playground](/playground).
H2[objections-you-will-hear-and-honest-answers]: Objections you will hear, and honest answers
UL:
- **'Customers hate talking to bots.'** They hate bots that trap them. They tend to accept an assistant that answers quickly, is clear that it is an AI and lets them reach a person in one step.
- **'It will say something wrong.'** It can, which is why grounding, testing and weekly review matter. Treat the knowledge base like a page on your website: accurate, dated and reviewed.
- **'We are too small.'** Small businesses gain the most, because the person answering enquiries is usually the owner, and every interruption costs billable time.
- **'It sounds robotic.'** Tone is a setting. Give it three sample replies in your own voice and it will follow them.
- **'What if it books the wrong thing?'** Confirmations state the service, time and price, and every booking can be changed or cancelled. Review the first fifty bookings.
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Leaving out-of-date prices in the documents.
- Letting the assistant answer medical, legal or financial questions.
- Not telling visitors it is an AI.
- Hiding the option to talk to a person.
- Measuring success by conversation volume instead of bookings and leads.
- Never reviewing transcripts.
H2[a-day-in-the-life-what-it-handles]: A day in the life: what it handles
P: To make this concrete, here is how a virtual AI receptionist might spend a normal day for a physiotherapy clinic that is closed on Sundays.
TABLE:
H: Time | What happens | What the receptionist does
R: Sunday 9 p.m. | A visitor asks whether the clinic treats sports injuries and how much a first session costs | Answers from the services page and price list and offers Monday morning slots
R: Sunday 9:10 p.m. | The visitor picks Monday 10:30 and gives a name and mobile number | Books, confirms by email and schedules a reminder
R: Monday 8 a.m. | Someone asks whether they need a referral | Answers from the policy document, or says it does not know and offers a message to the team
R: Monday 12 p.m. | A patient wants to move a Thursday appointment | Offers alternative times and updates the booking
R: Monday 3 p.m. | A visitor writes that they have severe pain and cannot move | Recognises urgency, tells them how to seek urgent care and alerts the team immediately
R: Monday 5 p.m. | The team reads the day's conversations | Finds three questions it could not answer and adds the answers to the knowledge base
P: Notice the last row. The receptionist improves every day because someone reads what customers asked. Without that review, the assistant stays as good as it was on day one.
H2[measuring-the-result]: Measuring the result
P: Pick a few numbers before launch and compare them after a month.
UL:
- **Enquiries answered within a minute,** compared with your previous response time.
- **Bookings from the website,** and how many were made outside office hours.
- **Missed or abandoned enquiries,** where visitors asked something and left without an answer.
- **Time your team spends on repetitive questions,** estimated before and after.
- **Handover requests and their causes,** which show what to add to the knowledge base or what genuinely needs a person.
P: If the numbers do not improve, the cause is usually content: missing answers, unclear prices, or no booking option. It is rarely the technology. See [how to review your chatbot conversations each week](/blog/audit-website-chatbot-conversations).
H2[questions-to-ask-before-you-choose]: Questions to ask before you choose
OL:
- Does it answer only from my documents, and does it say when it does not know?
- Can it book into my real calendar and prevent double bookings?
- How do I take over a conversation, and will I be notified?
- What does it cost at three times my volume?
- Where is my data stored, and can I delete it?
- Which channels does it cover today, and which are only planned?
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: What is a virtual AI receptionist?
A: It is software that handles the first layer of a front desk: answering common questions, capturing leads and booking appointments, at any hour, from your own content.
Q: Can it book appointments?
A: Yes, in products with booking. It shows real free slots from your calendar and confirms them, with protection against double booking.
Q: Will it replace my receptionist?
A: It usually takes over repetitive questions and after-hours enquiries, so your team can focus on people who need them. Judgement calls should stay with humans.
Q: How much does a virtual AI receptionist cost?
A: Expect a monthly subscription, from tens to a few hundred dollars for small businesses, plus AI usage. Compare the total against the value of the enquiries you would otherwise miss.
Q: Does it answer phone calls?
A: Some products do. Crescelo answers in website chat and messaging apps today; phone answering is under development.
Q: Is it safe to give it customer information?
A: Check the vendor's privacy policy and sub-processors, tell visitors they are talking to an AI, and collect only what you need.
