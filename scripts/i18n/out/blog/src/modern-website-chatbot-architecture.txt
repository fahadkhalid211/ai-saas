P: From the outside, an AI chat widget looks simple: a small bubble appears in the corner, you type a question and an answer streams back. Behind that bubble sits a chain of separate parts, each with a job, each able to fail or slow down, and each affecting speed, accuracy, cost and security. You do not need to be an engineer to benefit from understanding them. Knowing the parts helps you ask better questions when comparing products, diagnose problems ('the answers are slow' or 'it ignored my document'), decide where your data goes and judge claims made in marketing pages. This guide walks through the journey of one visitor's question from the click on your website to the answer on the screen, describing each component in plain language: the embed script, the widget, the chat server, retrieval from your documents, the AI provider, streaming, storage and the dashboard. It describes how Crescelo is put together where that is useful, and it flags where things commonly go wrong.
H2[the-journey-in-one-picture]: The journey in one picture
TABLE:
H: Step | What happens | Where it runs
R: 1. Page loads | Your website loads a small script | Visitor's browser
R: 2. Widget appears | The script fetches your assistant's settings and draws the chat bubble | Visitor's browser
R: 3. Visitor asks | The message is sent to the chat server | Visitor's browser to the platform's server
R: 4. Checks | The server checks the request is allowed and within limits | Platform server
R: 5. Retrieval | The server searches your documents for the best passages | Platform server and database
R: 6. Generation | The passages and the question go to the AI model, which writes the reply | AI provider
R: 7. Streaming | The reply is sent back word by word | Server to browser
R: 8. Storage | The conversation, and any lead or booking, is saved | Database
H2[1-and-2-the-embed-script-and-the-widget]: 1 and 2: the embed script and the widget
P: You paste one line of code into your website. It loads a script from the platform, which fetches the assistant's settings, such as name, colours, welcome message and starter questions, and draws the chat bubble. Two design choices matter here. First, the widget should not damage your site: Crescelo's widget draws itself inside a Shadow DOM, which keeps its styles separate from your page's, so your site's CSS cannot break the widget and the widget cannot restyle your site; see [why chat widgets use Shadow DOM](/blog/shadow-dom-website-chatbot-widgets). Second, it should be light, so it does not slow your page; see [performance of embedded chat widgets](/blog/performance-optimization-embedded-chat-widgets).
H2[3-and-4-the-request-and-the-checks]: 3 and 4: the request and the checks
P: When the visitor sends a message, the widget sends it over the internet to the platform's chat server. Before doing any work, a careful server checks the request:
UL:
- Is it for a real assistant that is switched on?
- Is the sender within rate limits, so one visitor or script cannot flood the service?
- Is the plan's monthly session allowance still available?
- Does the request look like abuse, such as a huge message or a bot?
- Which workspace owns this assistant, so data is kept apart from other customers?
P: These checks protect both you and the platform. They are also where much of the security work lives; see [keeping one customer's data away from another](/blog/multi-tenant-saas-chatbot-architecture).
H2[5-retrieval-finding-the-right-passages]: 5: retrieval, finding the right passages
P: This is where a grounded assistant differs from a general chatbot. Rather than answering from memory, the server searches your documents first. Your content was split into passages and indexed earlier, both by meaning (vector search) and by keywords. The question is used to find the most relevant passages, which are merged and reranked. See [reciprocal rank fusion](/blog/reciprocal-rank-fusion-rrf-rag). Recent messages are considered too, so a follow-up such as 'and on Saturdays?' is understood; see [multi-turn query condensation](/blog/multi-turn-query-condensation-rag).
TABLE:
H: If retrieval is good | If retrieval is poor
R: Answers quote your real prices and policies | The assistant misses information that is in your documents
R: It says it does not know when nothing matches | It may answer from irrelevant passages
R: Follow-ups make sense | Follow-ups lose the thread
H2[6-generation-the-ai-model-writes-the-reply]: 6: generation, the AI model writes the reply
P: The server sends the question, the recent conversation, the retrieved passages and your instructions to a language model at an AI provider such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Groq or DeepSeek. With bring-your-own-key, the request uses your account and your key, which the platform stores encrypted; see [bring your own AI key explained](/blog/bring-your-own-ai-key-byok-explained). The model writes the reply from the passages, following your rules about tone and topics. This step is the slowest and the main source of cost. It also has limits: a provider may be busy or rate limited, so a good platform can fall back to another model, and it should detect a reply that stops mid-sentence and ask the model to continue.
H2[7-streaming-the-answer-back]: 7: streaming the answer back
P: Instead of waiting for the whole reply, the server sends it to the browser as it is generated, so words appear almost immediately. This uses a one-way connection called Server-Sent Events; see [streaming replies: SSE versus WebSockets](/blog/sse-vs-websockets-ai-chatbot-streaming). It makes a multi-second answer feel quick.
H2[8-storage-and-actions]: 8: storage and actions
UL:
- **Conversation:** each message is saved so you can read the transcript and the visitor can continue where they left off.
- **Leads:** if the visitor gives contact details, the lead is saved and sent to your email, Slack, Google Sheets or a webhook.
- **Bookings:** if they book, the slot is locked, saved and confirmed by email.
- **Unanswered questions:** questions the assistant could not answer are logged so you can fill the gaps.
- **Usage:** sessions and AI usage are counted for your plan.

H2[where-things-go-wrong]: Where things go wrong
TABLE:
H: Symptom | Likely part | What to check
R: Widget does not appear | Embed script | Is the script installed and the domain allowed?
R: Slow first reply | Provider or retrieval | Try another model; check knowledge base size
R: Wrong or missing facts | Knowledge base and retrieval | Check the document, the wording and the unanswered list
R: Reply cut off | Provider limits | Modern platforms continue replies; report if it persists
R: Booking confusion | Booking settings | Check hours, slot length and confirmations
R: Invented answers | Instructions and grounding | Tighten instructions; test unknown questions
H2[questions-to-ask-any-vendor]: Questions to ask any vendor
UL:
- Where does my data live, and how is it separated from other customers?
- Which AI providers can I use, and can I bring my own key?
- How do you make sure answers come from my content?
- What happens when the AI provider is down?
- How are keys and secrets protected?
- Can I export my data and leave?
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: How does an AI website chatbot work?
A: A script loads a widget on your site; each question goes to a server, which searches your documents, sends the best passages to an AI model and streams the reply back.
Q: Where does the AI model run?
A: At the AI provider you choose, such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Groq or DeepSeek. Your documents are searched on the platform.
Q: Why do answers come from my documents?
A: Because retrieval finds relevant passages first and the model is instructed to answer from them, saying it does not know otherwise.
Q: Can the widget break my website?
A: A well-built widget isolates its styles and loads lightly. Crescelo's widget uses a Shadow DOM.
Q: What makes replies appear word by word?
A: Streaming: the server sends parts of the reply as they are generated.
Q: What do I control?
A: Your documents, instructions, provider and key, channels, integrations and team access.
