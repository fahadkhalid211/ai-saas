P: The path from 'I need a dentist' to 'I am in the chair' has more steps than most practices realise. A prospective patient asks whether you take new patients, wants a price, worries about insurance, books, receives forms, completes them, remembers to arrive and finally sits down. At each step some people drop away. Automation can smooth the early steps, the questions and the booking, while the sensitive steps, clinical history and identity, stay in secure systems. This guide sets out a sensible division for a dental practice: what a website chat can handle, what should move to secure forms, how booking and reminders fit, what privacy rules to respect and how to measure whether it is working.
NOTE: This is general information, not legal advice. Dental practices handle sensitive health information, and rules differ by country. Take advice about your obligations, and see our guide to [HIPAA considerations for dental chatbots](/blog/hipaa-compliance-dental-chatbots).
H2[the-new-patient-journey]: The new-patient journey
TABLE:
H: Stage | What the patient wants | Where it belongs
R: 1. Research | Are you accepting new patients? What do you offer? | Website and chat
R: 2. Price and insurance | What does a first visit cost? Do you take my insurance? | Chat, from approved documents
R: 3. Booking | A time that suits me | Chat or booking page
R: 4. Confirmation | Details of the visit and what to bring | Email confirmation
R: 5. Forms | Complete medical history and consent | Secure forms or your patient system
R: 6. Reminder | Do not forget the visit | Email or text reminders
R: 7. Arrival | Check in quickly | Front desk
P: Automating stages 1 to 4 and 6 is straightforward and low risk. Stage 5 is where sensitive information appears, and it should never sit in an open chat.
H2[what-a-website-chat-can-safely-handle]: What a website chat can safely handle
UL:
- **Whether you accept new patients,** opening hours, location, parking and accessibility.
- **A description of the first visit:** what happens, how long it takes and what to bring.
- **Fees you publish:** the new-patient exam, cleaning, X-rays and payment options.
- **Insurance information you can state:** which providers you work with, with a clear line that coverage must be confirmed with the insurer.
- **Booking a new-patient appointment** with minimal details: name and a way to reach the patient.
- **Directing people to secure channels** for forms and clinical questions.
P: A chat can do all this at any hour, from documents you approve. See [insurance and financing FAQ automation](/blog/dental-insurance-financing-faq-automation).
H2[what-should-stay-out-of-the-chat]: What should stay out of the chat
UL:
- Medical and dental history, medications and allergies.
- Symptoms in detail and photos of the mouth or face.
- Insurance member and group numbers.
- Government identifiers and payment card details.
- Anything a clinician needs to assess.
P: Give the assistant instructions to steer away from these and to say where they belong: 'For your medical history, we will send you a secure form after you book.' Add a notice in the chat asking visitors not to share health details there.
H2[booking-a-new-patient-visit]: Booking a new-patient visit
P: A first visit usually needs a longer slot than a check-up. With a tool that supports several appointment types, set a new-patient type with its own length. Crescelo's built-in picker uses one slot length, so choose the length of your standard new-patient exam, or use Calendly or Cal.com links for appointment types with different durations. Bookings arrive in your dashboard and a read-only calendar feed. See [AI appointment booking](/blog/ai-appointment-booking) and [configuring availability rules](/blog/configure-calendar-sync-availability-rules).
OL:
- The visitor asks about a first visit and gets an answer.
- The assistant offers real free times and books the visit.
- The confirmation email explains what to bring and how to change the booking.
- The confirmation also links to your secure new-patient forms, if you use them.
- A reminder goes out before the visit.
H2[secure-forms-and-the-handoff]: Secure forms and the handoff
P: Your practice management system or a secure forms provider should collect health history, consent and insurance details, behind proper access controls and with the agreements your rules require. Send the link in the confirmation, not in the chat transcript. If a patient tries to type their history in the chat, the assistant should politely redirect them. Ask any forms vendor about encryption, access controls and business associate or processing agreements.
H2[reminders-and-reducing-no-shows]: Reminders and reducing no-shows
P: New patients are more likely to forget or drop out than established ones. Send a confirmation immediately, a reminder a day or two before that repeats what to bring and includes a change link, and a last-minute nudge if you use texts. Remind them to complete forms in advance. See [how to reduce no-shows](/blog/eliminate-service-no-shows-playbook).
H2[consistent-honest-messaging]: Consistent, honest messaging
UL:
- Tell visitors they are talking to an AI assistant.
- Link to your privacy policy.
- Offer a route to a person, and say when the team is available.
- Do not make clinical promises, such as guaranteed outcomes or prices for treatment you have not assessed.
- Keep fee and insurance information current; out-of-date information is a common cause of complaints.
P: See [AI transparency and disclosure](/blog/ai-transparency-disclosure-building-trust).
H2[special-cases]: Special cases
H3: Anxious patients
P: Many people avoid the dentist because of fear. The assistant can respond warmly, explain what happens and mention any options you offer, without making clinical promises. See [pediatric dental inquiries](/blog/pediatric-dental-inquiries-chat) for a related example.
H3: Cosmetic consultations
P: People interested in whitening, veneers or implants want information before committing. Offer a consultation booking rather than quoting a treatment plan. See [cosmetic dentistry consultation booking](/blog/cosmetic-dentistry-consultation-booking).
H3: Emergencies
P: A visitor who describes an emergency should never be treated as a routine new patient. Use the safe routing described in [dental emergency triage with AI chat](/blog/dental-emergency-triage-ai-chat).

H2[a-sample-conversation]: A sample conversation
TABLE:
H: Speaker | Message
R: Visitor | Hi, I have just moved here and need a dentist. Are you taking new patients?
R: Assistant | Welcome to the area. Yes, we are welcoming new patients. A new-patient visit includes an exam, X-rays if needed and a chat about your goals, and takes about 60 minutes. Our fee is $95 without insurance. Would you like to see times this week?
R: Visitor | Thursday morning?
R: Assistant | Thursday has 9:00 and 10:30 free. Which suits you? I will need your name and a mobile number. After you book, we will email a secure link for your medical history, so please do not type health details here.
P: Notice that the assistant stated the fee, offered real times, asked only for booking details and explained where health information goes.
H2[front-desk-routine-after-launch]: Front desk routine after launch
OL:
- Each morning, check overnight bookings and messages.
- Confirm that new patients received the forms link, and follow up on any who have not completed it a day before the visit.
- Note the questions the assistant could not answer and add them to the knowledge base.
- Once a month, compare new-patient bookings, no-shows and form completion with the previous month.
P: This routine takes minutes and keeps the whole intake path reliable.
H2[common-mistakes]: Common mistakes
UL:
- Asking for medical history in the chat.
- Sending the forms link in an open message instead of the confirmation email.
- Using a slot length that is too short for a new-patient exam.
- Forgetting to update fees and insurers after changes.
- Never checking whether new patients complete forms before the visit.
- Not telling visitors they are talking to an AI.
P: Each of these is easy to avoid with a checklist, and the checklist is short: content approved, forms secure, booking length right, reminders on and conversations reviewed weekly.
H2[keep-the-human-touch]: Keep the human touch
P: Automation should make the first contact easier, not colder. A warm greeting, a clear explanation of the visit and an easy route to a person go a long way with people who are nervous about the dentist. Let the team introduce themselves in the confirmation email, and make sure a friendly voice answers when a patient calls.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: Can a chatbot handle new patient intake?
A: It can answer questions, share fees and insurance information you publish and book the visit. Medical history and consent should go through secure forms, not the chat.
Q: What should I not collect in a dental chat?
A: Health history, medications, detailed symptoms, insurance numbers and payment details.
Q: How do I get intake forms to patients?
A: Include a link to your secure forms in the booking confirmation, and remind patients to complete them before the visit.
Q: Can the assistant book different appointment types?
A: It depends on the tool. Crescelo's built-in picker uses one slot length; use Calendly or Cal.com links for several appointment types.
Q: Does this reduce front desk work?
A: It typically removes repetitive questions and after-hours enquiries, so the team can focus on patients in the practice.
Q: Is it safe to use a chatbot for a dental practice?
A: It can be, if you keep sensitive information out of the chat, tell visitors it is an AI, choose vendors carefully and follow the rules that apply to you.
