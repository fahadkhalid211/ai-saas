P: Customers reach a business in different ways. Some pick up the phone, especially when the problem is urgent or complicated. Others prefer typing, especially for quick questions and bookings, and especially outside working hours. An AI receptionist can serve either channel, and the two are not the same product: a voice assistant answers calls in real time, hears accents and background noise and speaks back, while a web chat assistant reads typed messages and shows buttons and booking cards. This guide compares them honestly: how customers behave, what each does well and badly, cost and complexity, privacy, urgency and the practical choice for a small service business. One point up front: Crescelo's assistant works in website chat and messaging apps today, and answering phone calls is under development and shown on the [roadmap](/roadmap), so we compare the approaches, not a product we do not yet offer.
H2[how-customers-choose-a-channel]: How customers choose a channel
TABLE:
H: Situation | Likely channel
R: Urgent problem: burst pipe, severe pain | Phone
R: Complex or emotional matter | Phone or in person
R: Quick factual question: price, hours | Chat or search
R: Booking outside office hours | Chat
R: Someone in a public place or at work | Chat
R: Older customers or those with difficulty typing | Phone
R: Customers who dislike phone calls | Chat
P: Look at how your customers contact you today, and ask them. A business whose customers overwhelmingly phone will not be helped much by chat alone, and one whose enquiries arrive through the website will not need a phone assistant first.
H2[what-voice-ai-does-well]: What voice AI does well
UL:
- Answers calls instantly, at any hour, without a queue.
- Feels natural to people who prefer talking.
- Handles urgent situations where typing is awkward.
- Can take messages, route calls and book appointments by voice.
H3: Where voice AI struggles
UL:
- **Accents, dialects and noise:** speech recognition errors on a phone line are common, especially with background sounds.
- **Names, addresses and numbers:** hard to capture accurately by ear; spelling back is clumsy.
- **Interruptions and pauses:** conversation timing is harder than turn-taking in chat.
- **Emotional calls:** an AI voice can feel cold to a distressed caller.
- **Latency:** delays before the AI answers feel awkward.
- **Recording and consent rules:** calls may be recorded, and rules vary by place.
H2[what-web-chat-does-well]: What web chat does well
UL:
- Text is exact: names, addresses and emails are captured as typed.
- Visitors can read at their own pace and scroll back.
- Rich elements: buttons, booking cards, links and images.
- Works quietly in public and at night.
- Easy to keep a transcript and review it.
H3: Where web chat struggles
UL:
- Some customers will not type or do not think of the website.
- Urgent callers may prefer to phone.
- Long conversations are slower than talking.
- Accessibility depends on the widget's quality.

H2[cost-and-complexity]: Cost and complexity
P: Voice needs more moving parts than chat: a phone number, call routing, speech recognition, a text-to-speech voice and low latency between them, and its usage cost is often measured in minutes. Chat needs content and a widget. For a small business, chat is usually cheaper and quicker to launch and easier to keep accurate. Voice can be worthwhile when phone volume is high and missing calls costs real money.
H2[privacy-and-consent]: Privacy and consent
UL:
- Calls may need a recording notice, and consent rules differ by country and state.
- Voice data is more sensitive than text, and voiceprints can be biometric data in some laws.
- You must tell callers they are speaking to an AI.
- Ask providers where audio is processed and stored, and how long.
- Chat transcripts are also personal data; see the [GDPR checklist for website chatbots](/blog/website-chatbot-gdpr-checklist).
H2[emergencies]: Emergencies
P: Voice is the natural channel for emergencies, and the riskiest for automation. A voice assistant must recognise urgent words reliably despite noise, give approved instructions and hand over to a person fast. Test with realistic accents and noisy recordings. For most small businesses, a human or a proper emergency line should own emergencies whatever the channel. See [handling emergencies in automated chat](/blog/handling-emergencies-automated-chat).
H2[a-practical-decision]: A practical decision
TABLE:
H: If | Then
R: Most enquiries come from the website and messaging | Start with web chat
R: Most enquiries are calls and you miss many | Add call answering, by an AI product you trust or a human service, alongside chat
R: Your calls are emotionally sensitive | Prefer people on the phone; use chat for simple admin
R: You need bookings out of hours | Chat covers it now; voice can follow
R: Budget is tight | Chat first
P: You do not have to choose forever. Many businesses start with chat, learn what customers ask, then add voice where the numbers justify it. The knowledge base you build for chat is the same content a voice assistant would need.

H2[worked-example-a-plumbing-company]: Worked example: a plumbing company
P: A plumbing company counted its enquiries: sixty percent phone, thirty percent website chat and form, ten percent messaging apps. Calls during jobs went to voicemail, and roughly a third of callers did not leave a message. The owner did two things: added a chat to the website for the thirty percent, and set up a call-answering service for the phone. He also put the chat link in the voicemail greeting: 'Or ask us online and get an instant reply.' Within a month the chat took a growing share of evening enquiries, and the missed-call problem was reduced by the answering service. The lesson is that the channels complement each other, and the shared knowledge base kept answers consistent.
H2[frequently-asked-questions]: Frequently asked questions
FAQ:
Q: Is a voice AI receptionist better than web chat?
A: Neither is better in general. Voice suits phone-first customers and urgent calls; chat is cheaper, more accurate for details and easier to launch.
Q: Does Crescelo answer phone calls?
A: Not yet. Phone answering is under development. Crescelo works in website chat, hosted pages, WhatsApp and Telegram.
Q: Why is voice AI less accurate for names and numbers?
A: Speech recognition can mishear names, addresses and digits, especially with accents and background noise.
Q: Do I need to tell callers it is an AI?
A: Yes, and many places have rules on disclosure and recording. Check what applies to you.
Q: Which is cheaper?
A: Chat usually is, because voice adds telephony and per-minute costs.
Q: Can I use both?
A: Yes. Many businesses use chat for the website and a separate solution for calls, with shared information.
